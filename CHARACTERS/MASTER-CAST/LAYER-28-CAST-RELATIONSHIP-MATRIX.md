# Layer 28 — Cast Relationship & Event Architecture

## Goal
Turn the expanded cast into an interconnected social ecosystem. Characters should meet, love, resent, protect, betray and grieve one another independently of the player's direct involvement.

## Relationship graph principles

### 1. No isolated characters
Every major character should connect to at least two other named characters through family, work, friendship, romance, conflict, debt, shared history or information.

### 2. Contradictory relationships
A relationship may contain multiple simultaneous states. Example: love + resentment + dependency.

### 3. Private relationships
Not every relationship is publicly known. Secret relationships can be discovered through diaries, overheard conversations, photographs or event attendance.

### 4. Relationships change through events
Weddings, birthdays, births, deaths, betrayals, injuries and discoveries can alter relationship states.

## Initial relationship seeds

- Betrayed Mother ↔ Unfaithful Husband — marriage under severe fracture.
- Unfaithful Husband ↔ Other Woman — secret affair with consequences.
- Teen Mother ↔ Young Father — unresolved relationship to be developed through later character work.
- Blonde Mean Girl ↔ Bad Boy — intense, mutually destructive attachment.
- Nun ↔ Young Priest — shared faith but different responses to danger.
- CEO ↔ Former Partner — complicated history and hidden intimacy.
- Beloved Icon ↔ Former Celebrity Assistant — public loyalty versus private knowledge.
- Beloved Icon ↔ Hated Truth-Teller — unresolved history and opposing public narratives.
- Mechanic ↔ Radio Technician — practical collaboration with conflicting priorities.
- Doctor ↔ Nurse — professional trust strained by scarcity decisions.
- Teacher ↔ Teacher's Sister — family connection across settlement boundaries.
- Photographer ↔ Musician — archive of community events.
- Librarian ↔ Journalist — evidence and historical records.
- Widower ↔ Grieving Brother — shared grief expressed differently.
- Cook ↔ Farmer — food supply dependency.
- Bus Driver ↔ Teacher — shared history involving school transport; potential conflict.

## Event architecture

### Wedding
Potential attendees can include characters from multiple relationship clusters. The event can alter trust, reveal secrets, create photographs and establish memories.

Possible outcomes:
- successful ceremony;
- interrupted ceremony;
- absent partner;
- public confession;
- private argument;
- disappearance;
- later death of an attendee;
- player absent but consequences still occur.

### Birthday
Can be child-focused or adult-focused. The event establishes normalcy, family bonds and photographs that may become emotionally important later.

Possible outcomes:
- celebration succeeds;
- gift reveals hidden information;
- attendee leaves early;
- argument outside the celebration;
- later discovery that someone photographed the wrong person or place.

### Birth
The Teen Mother or another pregnant character can give birth during the broader game. The event changes relationships, routines, inventory needs and mobility.

### Burial
Can be public, private, interrupted, delayed or missed. Strong attachments increase probability of private rituals.

## Off-screen event simulation

The world state should support events occurring while the player is elsewhere.

For each event:
1. Determine eligible participants.
2. Evaluate relationships and current location.
3. Select event outcome.
4. Update relationship states.
5. Create evidence/remnants where appropriate.
6. Allow player discovery later.

## Discovery artifacts
Events may leave:
- photographs;
- diaries;
- letters;
- clothing;
- food remnants;
- decorations;
- recordings;
- graves;
- gifts;
- damaged objects;
- witness accounts.

## Emotional design target
A player should be able to miss an event and later feel that something meaningful happened without them.

## Future implementation
This matrix should eventually feed a data-driven relationship/event system rather than hard-coded one-off sequences.
