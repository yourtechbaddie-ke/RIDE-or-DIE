# Ride or Die — Expanded Ensemble Cast Architecture

## Layer 28

### Purpose
Create the foundation for a living ensemble of approximately 50–60 named characters whose lives, relationships and choices can continue outside the player's immediate view.

This is an architecture document, not a final casting list. New characters can be added without breaking existing relationship or story systems.

## Character tiers

| Tier | Target | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Core | 6 | Central narrative anchors |
| Major recurring | 12 | Long-running personal arcs |
| Supporting | 18 | Interconnected human stories |
| Peripheral named | 14 | Local stories, events and discoveries |
| **Named total** | **50** | Initial production target |

Additional unnamed/ambient residents can populate settlements without requiring full authored arcs.

## Character record standard
Every named character should eventually have:

- stable ID;
- name;
- age range;
- appearance;
- voice profile;
- occupation/background;
- personality;
- values;
- fears;
- desires;
- secrets;
- relationships;
- family;
- daily routine;
- possessions;
- food/shelter needs;
- emotional states;
- violence threshold;
- injury behaviour;
- death conditions;
- survival conditions;
- diary/letter potential;
- event participation;
- chapter presence;
- branching consequences;
- ECHO relationship;
- possible ending states.

10. **The Other Woman** — not reduced to a villain; has her own circumstances, fears and consequences.

17. **The CEO's Former Partner** — knows the person behind the public persona and may hold compromising information.

## Important design rule
These are **story seeds, not fixed stereotypes**. Every character must gain contradictions, personal history and agency before final implementation.

## Relationship architecture
Relationships are directed and stateful. A character can feel affection toward another while simultaneously resenting them.

Recommended relationship dimensions:
- trust;
- affection;
- loyalty;
- resentment;
- fear;
- dependency;
- attraction;
- grief;
- debt;
- suspicion.

A single numerical relationship score is insufficient.

## Character autonomy
Important NPCs should be able to:
- leave a location;
- meet privately;
- form or break relationships;
- attend events;
- miss events;
- write personal records;
- hide information;
- help someone without the player knowing;
- commit an impulsive act;
- bury someone secretly;
- die off-screen when narrative conditions allow.

## Emotional continuity
A character who witnesses violence should not automatically return to their default idle state. Emotional residue should persist until processed through sleep, conversation, routine, grief, reconciliation or time.

## Death architecture
Deaths should support multiple states:
- witnessed;
- discovered later;
- reported by another character;
- inferred from absence;
- secretly concealed;
- buried;
- unburied;
- publicly memorialized;
- forgotten by the community but remembered by one person.

## Expansion rule
New character ideas supplied during production should be integrated by connecting them to existing characters, places, events or factions wherever possible. Avoid isolated one-off NPCs when an existing relationship can create a stronger story network.
