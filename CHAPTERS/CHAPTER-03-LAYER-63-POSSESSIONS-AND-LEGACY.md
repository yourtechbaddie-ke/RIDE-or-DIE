# CHAPTER 3 — THE BLACK HIGHWAY
## LAYER 63 — POSSESSIONS & LEGACY

### Purpose
Make objects part of the player's history. A life should leave physical evidence behind.

### Core Rule
Possessions are not loot by default. They are pieces of a life.

### Possession Categories
- Personal: clothing, jewelry, phones, photographs, books, cosmetics, keepsakes.
- Practical: tools, food, medicine, batteries, fuel, weapons where appropriate, equipment.
- Property: homes, vehicles, businesses, land and storage spaces.
- Sentimental: letters, gifts, family heirlooms, diaries and handmade objects.
- Information-bearing: maps, records, photographs, recordings and documents.

### Systems
- Items can be bought, inherited, gifted, traded, stolen, lost, destroyed or abandoned.
- Ownership can be disputed.
- NPCs recognize meaningful objects.
- Returning a dead person's possession can change a relationship.
- Keeping it can comfort, burden or implicate the player.
- Children can inherit objects and stories.
- A valuable object may be materially useful and emotionally priceless at the same time.
- Property can become a shelter, business, community hub or contested location.

### Death Continuity
When a character dies, their possessions remain in the world according to circumstances. The player may find a jacket, recipe book, car, photograph or unopened letter long after the character is gone.

### Material Progression
Survive → Earn → Acquire → Build → Lose → Adapt → Rebuild → Expand.

### Layer Lock
> “A life does not disappear when a person dies. Sometimes it remains in a house, a coat, a photograph, a car key—and in the hands of whoever finds it next.”
