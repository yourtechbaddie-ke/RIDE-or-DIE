# Chapter 2 — Layer 32: Emotional Memory & Consequence Engine

## Status
**Creative direction locked.**

## Purpose
Create the system that makes Ride or Die remember what happened to people, relationships and places—and allows those memories to change future behaviour.

The goal is not a simple morality meter. The goal is **human consequence**.

---

# 1. Core principle

> **People remember what happened to them. The world remembers what happened to people.**

A major action should not disappear when its scene ends.

Consequences can return through:
- dialogue;
- behaviour;
- relationships;
- routines;
- environmental changes;
- events;
- diaries;
- photographs;
- rumours;
- grief;
- fear;
- future decisions.

---

# 2. No universal morality score

Avoid reducing the protagonist to a single Good/Evil statistic.

Instead track contextual consequences such as:
- trust gained;
- trust lost;
- fear caused;
- loyalty earned;
- betrayal remembered;
- debt created;
- grief caused;
- information revealed;
- information concealed;
- promises kept;
- promises broken.

The same action can be interpreted differently by different characters.

---

# 3. Character memory

Named characters can remember meaningful interactions.

Memory categories:
- first meeting;
- kindness;
- humiliation;
- rescue;
- abandonment;
- betrayal;
- witnessed violence;
- shared secret;
- romantic moment;
- death of someone important;
- broken promise;
- gift received;
- event attended together.

Memories have emotional weight rather than simply existing as database flags.

---

# 4. Emotional residue

After major events, characters enter temporary or persistent emotional states.

Possible states:
- shock;
- grief;
- anger;
- guilt;
- relief;
- suspicion;
- attachment;
- fear;
- numbness;
- hope;
- exhaustion.

States can affect:
- voice;
- posture;
- dialogue choices;
- social distance;
- routines;
- willingness to help;
- sleep;
- appetite;
- risk tolerance.

---

# 5. Delayed consequences

Not every consequence happens immediately.

A character may appear calm after a betrayal and only break down hours later.

A survivor may continue working immediately after a death and grieve that night.

A relationship may appear unchanged until a later event forces the unresolved issue back into the open.

This supports the game's cinematic pacing.

---

# 6. Memory through objects

Objects can become memory anchors.

Examples:
- a wedding ring;
- a child's toy;
- a photograph;
- a cooking pot;
- a jacket;
- a diary;
- a birthday gift;
- a musical recording;
- a weapon associated with a traumatic event.

The object itself can remain unchanged while its meaning changes.

---

# 7. Environmental memory

Locations can retain state from previous events.

Examples:
- a kitchen after a violent argument;
- a bedroom after a death;
- a wedding venue after the celebration;
- a grave after burial;
- a child's room after the family leaves;
- a room rearranged after a birth.

This connects Layer 32 directly to Layer 30.

---

# 8. Relationship consequences

Relationships are multi-dimensional.

A character can:
- love someone and distrust them;
- forgive someone but remain afraid of them;
- resent someone while still protecting them;
- hate someone while respecting their courage;
- mourn someone who previously betrayed them.

Avoid binary relationship states wherever possible.

---

# 9. Death memory

When someone dies, surviving characters do not all react identically.

Possible reactions:
- grief;
- denial;
- anger;
- relief;
- guilt;
- silence;
- practical action;
- revenge;
- avoidance;
- secret burial;
- preserving belongings;
- destroying belongings.

A character's relationship to the deceased determines the response.

---

# 10. Secret burial consequences

A private burial can create its own memory chain.

The participant may:
- revisit the grave;
- hide the location;
- leave flowers;
- leave objects;
- confess to someone;
- deny the burial publicly;
- be caught;
- become emotionally attached to the location.

Another character may discover the grave much later.

---

# 11. Personal Paparazzi memory

Photographs from Layer 29 can become memories and evidence.

A photograph may:
- increase fear;
- establish a relationship;
- contradict testimony;
- reveal a missed event;
- identify a location;
- expose long-term surveillance;
- become a keepsake after the photographer dies.

A photograph can change meaning after the photographer's identity is revealed.

---

# 12. Diaries as delayed emotional triggers

A diary discovered after death can change the protagonist's interpretation of previous events.

The system should support retrospective realization rather than merely delivering new facts.

A player may remember:
- an argument;
- a strange kindness;
- an unexplained disappearance;
- a repeated phrase;
- a gift;

and reinterpret it after reading the diary.

---

# 13. Events create shared memories

Weddings and birthdays can create memories among attendees.

Characters can later remember:
- who attended;
- who was absent;
- who gave a speech;
- who left early;
- who caused an argument;
- who took photographs;
- who disappeared.

The same event may be remembered differently by different people.

---

# 14. Birth and family continuity

When a child is born during the game, the event changes more than dialogue.

Potential changes:
- sleeping arrangements;
- daily routines;
- supplies;
- mobility;
- relationship priorities;
- protective behaviour;
- fatigue;
- emotional stakes.

The Teen Mother can continue participating in the story while carrying or caring for her baby rather than disappearing from gameplay.

The child's existence should create both vulnerability and emotional continuity.

---

# 15. Rumour and incomplete information

Characters can remember events incorrectly.

Rumours can spread through:
- overheard conversations;
- community gatherings;
- workplaces;
- radio broadcasts;
- diaries;
- photographs.

A rumour is not automatically true.

The player should sometimes have to distinguish memory, testimony and evidence.

---

# 16. Player absence as a consequence

The player's absence is itself a state.

If the protagonist misses:
- a wedding;
- a birthday;
- a burial;
- a birth;
- a rescue;
- a conversation;

characters may remember that absence.

A character might later say nothing at all—but become emotionally colder.

---

# 17. Reconciliation and repair

Consequences should not always be permanent.

Characters can sometimes repair relationships through:
- honest conversation;
- actions;
- shared danger;
- returning an object;
- keeping a promise;
- attending an important event;
- protecting someone.

Repair should require believable effort.

---

# 18. Irreversible consequences

Some actions should remain irreversible.

Examples:
- death;
- certain betrayals;
- permanent injury;
- destroyed evidence;
- missed births or final goodbyes;
- exposure of a secret;
- abandonment at a critical moment.

This creates meaningful stakes without requiring every choice to branch the entire game.

---

# 19. Cinematic implementation

The system should surface consequences through scenes rather than dashboards.

Prefer:
- changed dialogue;
- changed behaviour;
- altered rooms;
- missing people;
- discovered objects;
- photographs;
- diaries;
- different event attendance;
- changed soundscapes.

Avoid constantly displaying numerical consequence values to the player.

---

# 20. Layer 32 acceptance criteria

- [ ] Meaningful actions create persistent memories.
- [ ] No universal morality score is required.
- [ ] Emotional residue can persist after major events.
- [ ] Consequences can be delayed.
- [ ] Objects can carry emotional meaning.
- [ ] Locations retain event history.
- [ ] Relationships support contradictory emotions.
- [ ] Death responses vary by relationship.
- [ ] Secret burials create continuing consequences.
- [ ] Paparazzi photographs can become evidence and memories.
- [ ] Diaries can trigger retrospective reinterpretation.
- [ ] Events create shared but subjective memories.
- [ ] Birth changes family routines and gameplay.
- [ ] Rumours can diverge from truth.
- [ ] Player absence can affect relationships.
- [ ] Reconciliation is possible where believable.
- [ ] Some consequences remain irreversible.
- [ ] Consequences are primarily communicated through the world rather than numerical UI.

## Direction lock
**Ride or Die does not ask the player whether an action was good or evil. It asks what that action did to people—and whether they will ever be the same afterward.**
