# CHAPTER 3 — THE BLACK HIGHWAY
## LAYER 70 — THE UNEXPECTED SHELTER

### Purpose
Expand the meaning of safety. A shelter is a temporary agreement between people, resources and circumstances—not a guaranteed safe zone.

### Core Rule
Every shelter has strengths, weaknesses and a human story.

### Shelter Types
- abandoned homes
- occupied houses
- roadside businesses
- farms
- churches and community buildings
- vehicles and temporary camps
- improvised roadside shelters
- controlled settlement facilities

### Shelter State
Track occupants, supplies, structural condition, access points, noise, visibility, sanitation, trust, leadership and external threats.

### Social Safety
A shelter may be physically secure but socially dangerous. Someone may steal, lie, abuse authority, betray the group or invite outsiders. Conversely, a seemingly dangerous stranger may become the safest person in the room.

### Player Choices
The player can share a shelter, claim it, negotiate access, reinforce it, leave quietly, warn occupants, hide resources or risk strangers entering.

### Changing Safety
Shelters evolve. A place that saved the player once can later become unsafe because supplies run out, people leave, violence arrives, weather changes, disease spreads or the organization discovers it.

### Emotional Spaces
Some shelters become meaningful homes. Players may cook, sleep, talk, repair possessions, care for children, grieve or celebrate there. These spaces can later be lost.

### Layer Lock
> “Safe is not a place. Safe is a moment when everything holding danger outside still holds.”
