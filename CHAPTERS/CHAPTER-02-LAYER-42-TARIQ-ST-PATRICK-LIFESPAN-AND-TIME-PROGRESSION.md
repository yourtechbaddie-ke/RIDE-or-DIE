# Chapter 2 — Layer 42: Tariq St. Patrick — Lifespan & Time Progression

## Status
**Creative direction locked.**

## Canon update
Tariq St. Patrick is the canonical name of the newborn introduced during the story.

Tariq is **not permanently a newborn character**. The game's timeline may advance far enough for the player to experience Tariq growing from infancy into childhood and, eventually, adolescence.

---

# 1. Core principle

> **Tariq is a person with a future, not a narrative prop.**

His existence should demonstrate that RIDE OR DIE's world can evolve over meaningful time.

---

# 2. Time progression

The game may support substantial time jumps or an extended timeline when narratively justified.

Potential life stages:

1. newborn;
2. infant;
3. toddler;
4. child;
5. pre-teen;
6. teenager.

The exact pacing is a future narrative decision and should never be artificially compressed merely to reach a gameplay milestone.

---

# 3. Tariq's growth changes gameplay

As Tariq grows, his capabilities and needs change.

### Newborn
- completely dependent on caregivers;
- requires feeding, warmth and protection;
- cannot navigate independently;
- affects adult movement and decision-making.

### Toddler
- beginning mobility;
- can recognize familiar people;
- can create unexpected noise or movement;
- can form early memories;
- requires supervision.

### Child
- greater mobility;
- can learn routines;
- can communicate more clearly;
- can notice environmental details;
- can develop relationships with other children and adults.

### Pre-teen
- increasing independence;
- stronger opinions;
- greater awareness of the Collapse and its consequences;
- can contribute to certain survival tasks appropriately.

### Teenager
- substantially greater autonomy;
- personal identity and values become more defined;
- can form independent relationships;
- can disagree with caregivers;
- can make meaningful choices that affect the wider story;
- can develop skills learned from the surviving community.

No child or teenager should be treated as an adult substitute merely for gameplay convenience.

---

# 4. Memory continuity

Tariq's understanding of earlier events should evolve with age.

He may:
- remember fragments;
- misunderstand events;
- ask questions about people who died;
- inherit photographs and objects;
- hear conflicting stories about his own birth;
- eventually discover truths that adults tried to hide.

The player may experience the emotional impact of realizing that **Tariq remembers parts of a world the player thought was already forgotten.**

---

# 5. Relationship continuity

Tariq's relationships evolve naturally.

He may form bonds with:
- his parent(s);
- caregivers;
- siblings if introduced;
- surviving relatives;
- other children;
- trusted adults;
- people who were present during his birth.

Relationships can strengthen, fracture or disappear as the Collapse changes the community.

---

# 6. Survival significance

Tariq remains part of the game's survival framework.

His age affects:
- mobility;
- food requirements;
- shelter requirements;
- medical vulnerability;
- travel decisions;
- risk calculations;
- caregiver availability;
- group dynamics.

As he becomes older, Tariq can gradually transition from someone who must be protected to someone who can participate in protecting others.

---

# 7. The Collapse across generations

Tariq provides a way to show the long-term consequences of The Collapse.

A player who survives long enough to see Tariq become a teenager should be able to recognize:

- places that changed;
- people who disappeared;
- traditions that survived;
- objects that became relics;
- stories that became myths;
- children who grew up too quickly;
- communities that rebuilt;
- consequences of the player's earlier choices.

The world should not look identical after years have passed.

---

# 8. Character development

Tariq's personality should not be predetermined as “the innocent child.”

His personality emerges from:
- genetics and temperament where appropriate;
- caregivers;
- environment;
- experiences;
- education;
- losses;
- friendships;
- player choices;
- community values.

Two playthroughs can produce different versions of Tariq.

---

# 9. Emotional payoff

A powerful long-term possibility is for the player to encounter Tariq as a teenager and realize:

**“I remember when he couldn't even walk.”**

That feeling should come from gameplay history, not a narrated montage.

The player may remember:
- carrying him;
- protecting him;
- finding food for him;
- hearing him cry as a newborn;
- watching him take his first steps;
- losing people around him;
- teaching him something;
- making decisions that shaped his childhood.

---

# 10. Important restraint

Tariq's growth is an optional long-form narrative capability, not a promise that every playthrough reaches adolescence.

The game may end earlier depending on:
- player choices;
- survival outcomes;
- timeline structure;
- campaign length;
- character survival.

If the story reaches adolescence, Tariq's progression must feel earned.

---

# 11. Layer 42 acceptance criteria

- [ ] Tariq St. Patrick is the canonical newborn name.
- [ ] Tariq is treated as a full character rather than a prop.
- [ ] The timeline can support growth beyond infancy.
- [ ] Newborn, toddler, child, pre-teen and teenage stages are supported conceptually.
- [ ] Behaviour remains developmentally appropriate.
- [ ] Age changes Tariq's gameplay role.
- [ ] Memories can evolve with age.
- [ ] Relationships can change across time.
- [ ] Tariq remains part of the survival system.
- [ ] The Collapse's long-term consequences can be experienced through his life.
- [ ] His personality can be shaped by the world and player history.
- [ ] Different playthroughs can produce different developmental outcomes.
- [ ] Reaching adolescence is not mandatory in every playthrough.

## Direction lock
**Tariq St. Patrick begins the story as a newborn. He does not have to remain one. If the player survives long enough, they may one day look across the changed world and see the baby they fought to keep alive standing there as a teenager.**
