# Chapter 3 — Layer 46: Pre-Collapse Life Window

## Status
**Canon direction locked.**

## Purpose
Make the characters feel alive before The Collapse without delaying the game's core survival identity.

## Core principle
> **Let the player fall in love with the world before taking the world away. But do not make them wait hours to discover the survival game.**

The pre-Collapse period is a **playable onboarding window**, not a separate life simulator and not a feature-length prologue.

## 1. Target pacing

The pre-Collapse experience should be intentionally compact and varied.

The player should understand within the early portion of the game that:
- these people have ordinary lives;
- this world is worth caring about;
- the player can physically inhabit it;
- something is becoming increasingly wrong;
- survival gameplay is coming.

The exact runtime remains a production/balancing decision, but the design principle is firm: **do not spend several hours on peaceful life before introducing meaningful Collapse pressure.**

## 2. Seamless morning routines

Characters can begin playable segments by waking in their actual environment.

Possible routines:
- waking in bed;
- checking a phone;
- opening curtains;
- showering;
- brushing teeth;
- dressing;
- preparing breakfast;
- making coffee/tea;
- feeding children;
- preparing school/work items;
- tidying a room;
- leaving home.

These actions should be interactive where they add character or gameplay value, not mandatory chores every morning.

## 3. Domestic realism

Homes should communicate character through behaviour and objects.

Examples:
- dishes left overnight;
- children's toys;
- laundry;
- meal preparation;
- television left running;
- family photographs;
- medication;
- work equipment;
- pets where appropriate;
- unfinished projects;
- personal messages.

The player learns who someone is by inhabiting their routine.

## 4. Cooking and eating

Cooking can be a lightweight interactive system during pre-Collapse life.

Players may:
- prepare ingredients;
- cook meals;
- plate food;
- eat;
- share meals with other characters;
- leave food unfinished;
- clean up.

Cooking should later connect naturally to survival systems after The Collapse.

## 5. Television and media

Characters may watch television, films or other media as part of ordinary life.

This should serve two purposes:
1. establish personality and culture;
2. provide diegetic early warning about the world.

A character may casually watch a news broadcast that contains an early Collapse signal.

The player can miss it.

Later, they may realize that the warning was there all along.

## 6. Chores and ordinary tasks

Optional everyday activities can include:
- washing dishes;
- laundry;
- cleaning;
- shopping;
- preparing bags;
- taking children somewhere;
- filling a vehicle;
- checking mail;
- walking through neighbourhoods.

These activities should be selectively interactive rather than turning the game into a chore simulator.

## 7. Character sampling

The player should not need to play lengthy routines for all 40+ characters before The Collapse.

Instead, the game introduces characters through carefully selected playable encounters.

Some characters may be experienced directly.

Others may be encountered through:
- shared events;
- workplaces;
- family connections;
- neighbourhood interactions;
- photographs;
- television;
- conversations;
- social gatherings.

This creates a broad cast without destroying pacing.

## 8. Events as introduction hubs

Major pre-Collapse events can efficiently introduce multiple characters.

Examples:
- wedding;
- birthday party;
- family gathering;
- workplace event;
- school event;
- celebrity appearance;
- dinner;
- community gathering.

The player can naturally meet several characters without requiring separate tutorial chapters for each.

## 9. Early signs of The Collapse

Peaceful gameplay gradually contains anomalies.

Examples:
- a delayed delivery;
- a news interruption;
- a power flicker;
- an unusual emergency alert;
- missing products at a shop;
- traffic disruption;
- a strange radio report;
- an unexplained communication failure;
- a character mentioning something unusual.

These should begin subtly and become harder to dismiss.

## 10. Pacing escalation

The pre-Collapse window follows an escalation pattern:

**Ordinary → Slightly strange → Uneasy → Disrupted → Dangerous → Collapse.**

The transition should feel earned rather than abrupt, while still arriving early enough to establish the survival genre.

## 11. Player attachment without false safety

The peaceful period should not imply that every introduced character is guaranteed to survive.

Players should gradually understand that everyone is vulnerable.

The game can foreshadow this through:
- health issues;
- relationship conflicts;
- risky routines;
- environmental warnings;
- character-specific secrets.

## 12. Reusable routines after The Collapse

Pre-Collapse interactions should establish mechanics that later become survival mechanics.

Examples:

**Cooking** → food preparation under scarcity.

**Driving** → dangerous travel with limited fuel.

**Shopping** → scavenging abandoned stores.

**Showering** → water scarcity.

**Watching television** → information scarcity after broadcasts fail.

**Sleeping safely** → choosing and securing shelter.

**Doing laundry** → clothing/resource management.

This makes the early life section mechanically relevant rather than disposable.

## 13. The emotional inversion

The same action should feel different before and after The Collapse.

A player may initially cook breakfast because it is routine.

Later, cooking breakfast may require:
- finding ingredients;
- rationing fuel;
- deciding who gets fed;
- protecting the cooking location from attracting attention.

The player experiences the transformation rather than being told that civilization changed.

## 14. Player freedom

Routine activities should be optional unless required for a specific mission or character introduction.

The game should never force the player to spend long periods:
- showering;
- washing dishes;
- watching television;
- eating;
- cleaning.

The goal is **believability and agency**, not simulation for simulation's sake.

## 15. Genre clarity safeguard

Within the opening experience, the player should receive unmistakable evidence that RIDE OR DIE is a survival game.

This can occur through:
- early survival tutorial mechanics;
- escalating shortages;
- an initial emergency sequence;
- environmental danger;
- a playable escape;
- resource pressure;
- the first serious survival decision.

The player should never reasonably conclude after several hours that the game is primarily a domestic-life simulator.

## 16. Canonical design statement

> **The pre-Collapse world is short enough to preserve momentum and rich enough to create attachment. The player learns to live before learning how to survive.**

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Characters can wake and begin routines in their actual environments.
- [ ] Showering, dressing, breakfast and household activities can appear naturally.
- [ ] Cooking and eating can be interactive without becoming mandatory busywork.
- [ ] Television and media can carry diegetic world information.
- [ ] Optional chores contribute to character realism.
- [ ] The 40+ cast can be introduced efficiently.
- [ ] Weddings, birthdays and gatherings can serve as character hubs.
- [ ] Early Collapse warnings appear before full disaster.
- [ ] Escalation moves from ordinary life toward danger.
- [ ] Pre-Collapse actions connect mechanically to later survival systems.
- [ ] Routine activities remain optional when not narratively required.
- [ ] The game establishes its survival identity early.

## Direction lock
**We show enough ordinary life to make players care—but never enough to make them forget that RIDE OR DIE is coming for the world.**
