# CHAPTER 3 — THE BLACK HIGHWAY
## LAYER 82 — THE MESSAGE NOBODY HEARD

### Purpose
Introduce missed communication as a grounded source of tragedy, discovery and alternate outcomes.

### Core Rule
A message only matters if it reaches someone—and many messages never do.

### Communication States
Messages may be delivered, delayed, overheard, intercepted, misunderstood, corrupted, ignored, erased or discovered years later.

### Formats
Phone calls, texts, radio transmissions, handwritten notes, voicemails, emails, recordings, public announcements, photographs and physical signs can all carry information.

### Consequences
A missed message can cause someone to stay, leave, wait, travel, hide, trust the wrong person or fail to reach a loved one.

### Player Agency
The player may choose whether to send, relay, hide, alter, destroy or ignore information. The player cannot guarantee that another person receives or believes it.

### NPC Autonomy
NPCs can send messages without the player. They may make desperate calls, leave notes, broadcast warnings, ask for help or intentionally remain silent.

### Investigation Connection
Old communications can become evidence in the Selection List and preparation mystery. Contradictory timestamps and incomplete transmissions can reveal that people were coordinating before the public understood what was happening.

### Emotional Payoff
A message discovered after a character's death can change what the player believed about that person's final hours without undoing the death.

### Echo Aurora
Echo-related interference may occasionally distort communications, but it remains an anomaly rather than a universal explanation for missing messages.

### Layer Lock
> “Sometimes the difference between finding someone and losing them is a message that arrived five minutes too late.”

> “And sometimes the message was sent. You just never heard it.”
