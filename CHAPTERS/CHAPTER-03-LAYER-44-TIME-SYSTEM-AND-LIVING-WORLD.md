# Chapter 3 — Layer 44: Time System & Living World

## Status
**Canon direction locked.**

## Purpose
Establish time as a playable world system so RIDE OR DIE evolves before, during and after The Collapse rather than freezing characters, locations or relationships in place.

## 1. Core principle

> **Time moves whether the player is looking or not.**

The world is not a static stage. Characters age, relationships change, businesses open and close, children grow, settlements evolve, infrastructure deteriorates or is repaired, and consequences accumulate.

## 2. Pre-Collapse life

The opening portion of the experience allows players to inhabit ordinary life through gameplay rather than a single exposition sequence.

Players can encounter:
- work;
- school;
- birthdays;
- weddings;
- restaurants;
- family homes;
- friendships;
- romance;
- arguments;
- celebrations;
- secrets;
- travel;
- celebrity appearances;
- ordinary routines.

The length of this period may vary by story route, but its purpose is to make the world and its people personally meaningful before survival pressure escalates.

## 3. The Collapse changes the clock

Once The Collapse begins, time becomes increasingly important to survival.

Possible pressures include:
- daylight and darkness;
- changing weather;
- resource depletion;
- injuries requiring recovery;
- food spoilage;
- power availability;
- travel windows;
- changing threats;
- community movement;
- emergency events.

## 4. Characters age

Relevant characters age naturally over the timeline.

This applies to:
- protagonists;
- parents;
- children;
- partners;
- friends;
- enemies;
- supporting characters;
- future generations.

Tariq St. Patrick is therefore part of a wider aging system, not an isolated exception.

## 5. Life-stage transitions

Age transitions can affect:
- appearance;
- voice;
- movement;
- relationships;
- responsibilities;
- skills;
- dialogue;
- emotional development;
- available activities;
- survival contribution.

The game should avoid treating aging as a simple numerical stat.

## 6. World-state progression

Locations can evolve independently.

Examples:
- a restaurant becomes a shelter;
- a school becomes an evacuation centre;
- a hotel becomes contested territory;
- a family home is abandoned;
- a road becomes blocked;
- a neighbourhood becomes a settlement;
- a settlement declines after resource loss;
- nature reclaims abandoned structures.

The player's actions can accelerate, prevent or redirect some changes.

## 7. Relationships change over time

Relationships should not remain permanently fixed.

Characters can:
- fall in love;
- separate;
- reconcile;
- marry;
- have children;
- become estranged;
- form new friendships;
- develop rivalries;
- forgive;
- refuse to forgive;
- grieve;
- move on.

These changes may occur because of player decisions or because of autonomous world events.

## 8. Births and new generations

The universe can introduce new characters through births as well as through migration, discovery and ordinary social expansion.

A newborn can eventually become a child, teenager and adult if the timeline supports it.

Not every child becomes a major character.

## 9. Death and absence

A character who dies leaves an absence that the world must respond to.

Examples:
- an empty chair at dinner;
- an unoccupied bedroom;
- a business without its owner;
- a family member changing routine;
- a memorial appearing;
- a diary being discovered later;
- a child remembering them years afterward.

## 10. Time skips

The story may use controlled time skips when appropriate.

A time skip must preserve continuity and show meaningful world changes rather than functioning as a shortcut that erases consequences.

Potential changes after a major skip:
- character ages;
- buildings;
- relationships;
- technology availability;
- settlement structures;
- resource conditions;
- political/social organization;
- memories and legends.

## 11. Player agency versus simulation

The world should feel alive without making every important event happen off-screen.

Major character deaths, births, betrayals and relationship milestones should generally have opportunities for player interaction when they are central to the player's route.

Background characters can have autonomous lives beyond the player's immediate view.

## 12. Information drift

As time passes, information can become distorted.

Later characters may hear different versions of earlier events.

A historical truth can become:
- a family story;
- a rumour;
- a photograph without context;
- a false public narrative;
- a diary account;
- a survivor testimony.

The player can sometimes know more than later generations because they personally experienced the original event.

## 13. Echo Aurora continuity

Echo Aurora remains part of the universe and can persist across time.

Its meaning may evolve as characters interpret new manifestations and later generations inherit incomplete records.

It should not be casually rewritten into a different phenomenon merely because the timeline advances.

## 14. The eight established regions

The existing world structure remains intact:

1. The Last City
2. The Black Highway
3. Hollowgate
4. The Verdant
5. The Ashen Expanse
6. The Drowned
7. The Silent North
8. The Origin

These regions can change over time without being replaced by new worlds unless future canon explicitly expands the universe.

## 15. Long-term emotional payoff

A player can return to a location years later and recognize what it used to be.

A child can become an adult.

A wedding photograph can become a historical artifact.

A survivor can tell a younger character about someone the player personally knew.

A home can become a ruin.

A ruin can become a settlement.

The player should feel that the world has a memory.

## 16. Game, not movie

Time progression must create gameplay opportunities.

Players should interact with changing systems rather than merely watch montages.

Examples:
- returning to a location after a season changes its traversal;
- choosing when to travel because darkness increases risk;
- preparing for a child's changing needs;
- deciding whether to wait for an injured companion to recover;
- discovering that a previously available resource has disappeared;
- revisiting an old character and finding their life transformed.

## 17. Canonical design statement

> **RIDE OR DIE is a living universe. The player experiences a world that existed before The Collapse, survives as it breaks, and can continue changing long afterward. Time belongs to everyone—not only the player.**

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Pre-Collapse life is playable.
- [ ] Time progresses throughout the world.
- [ ] Relevant characters age.
- [ ] Children can grow through life stages.
- [ ] Tariq participates in the universal aging system.
- [ ] Relationships evolve.
- [ ] Births and new generations are possible.
- [ ] Death leaves persistent consequences.
- [ ] Locations evolve.
- [ ] Controlled time skips preserve continuity.
- [ ] Later generations can inherit incomplete or conflicting histories.
- [ ] Echo Aurora remains canon.
- [ ] The eight established regions remain canon.
- [ ] Time creates gameplay consequences rather than cinematic montages alone.

## Direction lock
**The world does not pause when the player leaves the room.**
