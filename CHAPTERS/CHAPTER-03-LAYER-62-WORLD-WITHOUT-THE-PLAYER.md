# CHAPTER 3 — THE BLACK HIGHWAY
## LAYER 62 — WORLD WITHOUT THE PLAYER

### Purpose
Lock the principle that RIDE OR DIE continues even when the player is not present.

### Core Rule
The player is part of the world, not the clock that controls it.

### Autonomous World Events
- NPCs travel, work, trade, argue, reconcile, fall in love, separate and relocate.
- Children age and routines change.
- Food, fuel, medicine and money move through communities.
- Buildings change ownership or purpose.
- Vehicles are repaired, stolen, abandoned or repurposed.
- Settlements can grow, fracture or disappear.
- Characters can die away from the player.
- A rescue attempt can succeed before the player arrives—or fail before they know it happened.
- Rumors and information can spread without player participation.
- Organizations continue their operations while the player pursues other goals.

### Time and State
Important world events use persistent state rather than requiring the player to witness them. The game records cause, participants, location, time window and consequences.

### Player Discovery
The player may later discover an empty home, a changed business, a new grave, a moved family, a repaired vehicle, a diary entry or a survivor who says, “You missed it.”

### Fairness
The world never kills or changes important characters arbitrarily just to surprise the player. Major off-screen outcomes require traceable causes and sufficient world-state support.

### Generational Continuity
A child can become an older character while the player is elsewhere. Family stories, possessions, debts and reputations can survive the original characters.

### Layer Lock
> “The world does not wait for the player.”

> “Sometimes the most important thing that happened was the thing you were not there to see.”
