# CHAPTER 3 — THE BLACK HIGHWAY
## LAYER 97 — CONTINUATION AFTER LOSS

### Purpose
Define how gameplay continues naturally after the death of a currently experienced character.

### The World Chooses Continuity
There is no single universal replacement character.

The next playable perspective depends on the living state of the world:
- a nearby person
- a family member
- a companion
- someone connected through the current location
- another active thread
- a character whose story has become accessible through prior choices

### No Forced Replacement
The next perspective is not presented as “the new protagonist.”

There are no protagonists in the cast hierarchy.

The player simply gains access to another living person whose life is already unfolding.

### Emotional Transition
The transition can be immediate, delayed, quiet or discovered through the environment.

Examples:
- the player watches the aftermath before control returns
- another person searches for the deceased
- a family member receives the news
- a nearby survivor continues the journey
- an existing relationship becomes playable

### Knowledge Continuity
The player may know things the newly experienced character does not.

This creates an important distinction between:
- **player knowledge**
- **character knowledge**
- **world knowledge**

The player can choose whether to act on what they know when the new perspective has no in-world reason to know it.

### Permanent Loss
The deceased character remains dead unless a canonically established event has already created a genuine ambiguity.

There is no automatic resurrection and no save-state reversal disguised as story.

### Layer Lock
> “You lost the person. You did not lose the world.”

> “Someone else was still alive. Somewhere, life was still moving.”
