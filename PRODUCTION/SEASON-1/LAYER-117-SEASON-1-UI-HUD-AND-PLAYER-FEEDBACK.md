# LAYER 117 — SEASON 1 UI/HUD & PLAYER FEEDBACK

## Purpose

The interface should support survival without turning RIDE OR DIE into a menu-heavy RPG. UI communicates information while preserving immersion and uncertainty.

## Principles

- Minimal when information is not needed.
- Clear when an action matters.
- Context-sensitive rather than permanently cluttered.
- Diegetic presentation where appropriate.
- No excessive quest-marker dependency.
- Player agency remains visible without revealing every hidden system.

## Core Feedback

The player may receive readable feedback for:

- health and injury
- stamina or exertion
- relevant survival conditions
- inventory
- equipped objects
- nearby interactables
- navigation
- communication
- vehicle condition
- immediate danger
- important world-state consequences

## Discovery

Evidence, clues, rumors and secrets should not automatically appear as objective checklists. Players can discover information through observation, dialogue, objects, broadcasts and environmental details.

## Interaction Prompts

Prompts should identify meaningful actions without turning every object into an icon. Context and proximity determine available interactions.

## Death & Continuation

When a playable perspective dies, the interface must support a clear transition without implying a conventional game-over/reset. The new perspective enters an already-changing world.

## Reputation & Relationships

Important relationship and reputation changes may be communicated subtly through dialogue, behavior, access, reactions and contextual interface elements rather than numerical meters everywhere.

## Economy

Money, ownership, barter and resources may be represented clearly while avoiding an arcade-like presentation. Material progression should feel physical and consequential.

## ECHO Interface Events

ECHO manifestations may temporarily affect interfaces through corrupted displays, impossible readings or anomalous behavior. These events must remain rare and visually distinct.

## Canonical Rule

> The interface should tell the player what they need to act — not tell them everything the world knows.
