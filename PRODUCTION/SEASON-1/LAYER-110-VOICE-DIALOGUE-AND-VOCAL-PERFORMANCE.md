# LAYER 110 — VOICE, DIALOGUE & VOCAL PERFORMANCE

## Purpose

RIDE OR DIE is a human story before it is a survival system. Voice performance must make every named character feel like a complete person whose life exists beyond the current scene.

## 1. No Generic Voices

Every recurring named character receives a distinct vocal identity shaped by:

- age and life experience
- personality
- education and social environment
- region/cultural background
- emotional habits
- confidence or insecurity
- occupation
- relationships
- physical condition
- circumstances of the Collapse

Voice must never become a caricature of ethnicity, class, disability, wealth, or personality.

## 2. Dialogue Principles

Characters do not speak only to deliver quests or exposition. They can:

- interrupt one another
- hesitate
- change subjects
- joke
- lie
- misunderstand
- refuse to answer
- remember incorrectly
- speak privately
- reveal information accidentally
- remain silent
- talk about ordinary life
- discuss possessions, food, work, family and relationships
- react differently depending on who is present

Important information may emerge naturally rather than through exposition dumps.

## 3. Emotional Performance

The same sentence may sound different depending on fear, grief, anger, affection, exhaustion, embarrassment, suspicion, relief, attraction, guilt, or determination.

Performance states include:

- ordinary conversation
- private vulnerability
- public composure
- escalating fear
- panic
- controlled anger
- grief
- shock
- disbelief
- exhaustion
- physical pain
- relief
- affection
- playful teasing
- intimacy
- betrayal
- emotional shutdown

Silence, breathing, broken speech and unfinished sentences are valid performances.

## 4. Relationship-Aware Dialogue

Dialogue changes according to relationships. Characters may speak differently to:

- parents
- children
- partners
- former partners
- friends
- strangers
- authority figures
- enemies
- people they secretly love
- people they distrust
- people they believe are dead

NPCs remember important conversations when appropriate.

## 5. Rey & Tariq — Hidden Biological Connection

The Rey/Tariq relationship must be handled as a slow emotional mystery.

Rey is not naturally barren. Her infertility belief originates from a childbirth-related complication and the concealed removal of her newborn son immediately after birth.

Tariq was raised by wealthy adoptive parents. He does not initially know that Rey is his biological mother.

Before the truth is revealed, Rey and Tariq may naturally become close friends.

Rey's natural maternal instincts can make her unusually attentive to Tariq without either character consciously understanding why. This must remain emotionally believable rather than supernatural.

Potential performance details:

- Rey instinctively notices when Tariq is hungry, cold, frightened or overwhelmed.
- She may protect him before thinking about why.
- Tariq may feel unusually safe around Rey.
- Their conversations can develop a relaxed familiarity.
- Rey may recognize tiny mannerisms without consciously identifying their origin.
- Tariq's dimples are a locked visual identity feature and may become an emotionally important clue.
- Other characters may notice similarities before Rey does.

The eventual revelation must recontextualize earlier conversations rather than invalidate them.

## 6. Age & Vocal Continuity

Characters may mature without becoming unrecognizable.

Young characters should retain recognizable vocal identity as they mature, while gaining subtle changes in pitch, confidence, vocabulary, rhythm and emotional control.

Tariq may later appear as a teenager while retaining his recognizable facial features, including his dimples, and a believable continuity of voice and mannerisms.

Season 1 does not require every young character to undergo dramatic aging.

## 7. Autonomous NPC Conversation

NPCs may speak when the player is not directly involved. These conversations can reveal:

- changing relationships
- rumors
- resource shortages
- private fears
- plans
- betrayals
- celebrations
- grief
- ordinary life continuing despite disaster

The player should sometimes overhear a conversation without being its intended audience.

## 8. Voice Continuity Through Death

When a character dies, their voice does not become disposable. Recordings, voicemails, broadcasts, diaries, remembered phrases and other media may preserve their presence.

Their death can change how surviving characters speak about them.

## 9. Performance Direction

Actors should prioritize truthful human behavior over melodrama. The goal is not constant screaming, crying or dramatic declarations.

A quiet sentence delivered correctly can carry more emotional weight than a speech.

## 10. Canonical Rule

> In RIDE OR DIE, characters should sound like people who had entire lives before the player met them — and who continue having lives after the player leaves.
