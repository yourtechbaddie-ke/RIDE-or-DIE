# RIDE OR DIE — REALISM ASSET QUALITY LOCK

## Purpose

RIDE OR DIE is being produced as a believable living universe, not as a collection of game-looking characters placed inside game-looking environments.

This document raises the visual production bar for every human, NPC, location, prop, vehicle and world image.

## Non-Negotiable Visual Standard

Every visible asset must feel as though it exists in the same physically coherent world as the player.

The target is **cinematic photorealism with authored game readability** — not plastic 3D, generic AI art, stock-photo composition or exaggerated AAA character archetypes.

### Human realism

- Realistic facial anatomy with natural asymmetry.
- Real skin response: pores, fine texture, subtle pigmentation, small imperfections and believable subsurface scattering.
- Eyes must have believable wetness, depth, sclera variation and natural gaze behavior.
- Teeth must not appear uniformly manufactured.
- Hair must contain individual strands, clumping, flyaways, scalp variation and physically plausible movement.
- Ears, hands, nails, necks and feet receive the same realism attention as faces.
- Body proportions follow real human anatomy and the character's canonical age.
- No mannequin posture.
- No permanently perfect smiles.
- No identical neutral facial expression across NPCs.
- No duplicated faces, hair silhouettes or body shapes.

## Natural Human Behavior

Visual realism is incomplete without behavioral realism.

NPCs should:

- shift weight while standing;
- glance toward nearby movement;
- blink at non-uniform intervals;
- look away during uncomfortable conversation;
- interrupt and overlap dialogue naturally;
- check phones, bags, watches and surroundings when contextually appropriate;
- sit with imperfect posture;
- eat, drink, cough, yawn, stretch and fidget naturally;
- react differently to strangers, friends and threats;
- become distracted by events outside the player's view;
- change pace according to fatigue, injury, urgency and emotional state;
- avoid staring directly at the player without reason;
- maintain personal space;
- acknowledge environmental discomfort such as heat, rain, cold, smoke and noise.

Idle animation must communicate personality and circumstance rather than simply looping a generic animation.

## Clothing Realism

Clothing must behave like clothing.

Required details include:

- fabric weight;
- wrinkles and compression;
- seams and stitching;
- realistic layering;
- dirt and moisture accumulation;
- wear at elbows, knees, cuffs and footwear;
- weather-appropriate choices;
- occupation-appropriate choices;
- wardrobe rotation;
- repairs and improvised alterations after The Collapse.

No character should appear to own an infinite supply of perfectly clean identical outfits.

## Environment Realism

World images and 3D environments must communicate **place, time, population, economics, climate and history**.

Every environment should contain ordinary evidence of human life before it becomes an apocalypse set:

- partially used products;
- receipts and packaging;
- laundry;
- dishes;
- personal photographs;
- school/work materials;
- vehicle clutter;
- maintenance notices;
- imperfect signage;
- bins and recycling;
- construction differences;
- weathering;
- utility infrastructure;
- commercial branding;
- public transport details;
- believable pedestrian routes.

After The Collapse, these details do not simply disappear. They become abandoned, repurposed, damaged, scavenged, repaired, stolen or preserved according to the location's history.

## Lighting & Atmosphere

Lighting must be physically motivated.

Use:

- believable daylight direction;
- interior practical lights;
- window bounce;
- atmospheric haze where appropriate;
- wet-surface reflections;
- localized emergency lighting;
- changing weather illumination;
- realistic night exposure;
- motivated vehicle and street lighting.

Avoid universal blue/orange grading and generic "apocalypse" color filters.

## Camera & Composition

World images should look like frames from the actual universe, not concept-art posters.

Camera placement must respect physical space. People should have plausible eye lines, feet should meet surfaces, vehicles should contact roads correctly, and objects should have believable scale.

## AI Asset Quality Gate

An asset fails review if it contains:

- warped anatomy;
- repeated faces;
- unreadable or invented text used as critical signage;
- impossible shadows;
- floating objects;
- inconsistent reflections;
- melted hands;
- duplicated accessories;
- excessive skin smoothing;
- generic NPC expressions;
- fantasy-like environmental proportions without narrative justification;
- a visibly different rendering style from neighboring assets.

## Continuity

Every approved asset receives a continuity identity covering:

**Character/Location ID → canonical appearance → age/state → wardrobe → lighting context → damage/weather state → asset version.**

A later asset may improve fidelity, but it must not silently change established identity.

## Production Target

The player should be able to pause a frame and believe:

> "These are real people who happen to be living through this world."

That is the standard.
