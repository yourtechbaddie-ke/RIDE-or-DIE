# LAYER 109 — MUSIC & SCORE BIBLE

## Purpose
Define a distinctive musical identity for Season 1 that can carry ordinary life, dread, intimacy, survival, grief, mystery and the final hospital ambiguity.

## Musical Philosophy
The score should feel cinematic without turning every moment into a trailer. Music earns its strongest presence when emotion or story requires it.

## Musical States
Potential score families include:
- ordinary life;
- intimacy and attachment;
- discovery;
- unease;
- first warning;
- Collapse onset;
- escape/travel;
- hide-and-hunt;
- survival tension;
- grief;
- temporary safety;
- reunion;
- betrayal;
- investigation;
- Selection/organization mystery;
- Collector/Vera tension;
- Echo Aurora anomaly;
- quiet recovery;
- final Collapse;
- world-afterward;
- observation;
- hospital awakening;
- final impossible clue.

## Character Motifs
Recurring characters may receive subtle musical identities, but no character should be musically treated as the permanent "hero." Motifs can transform, fracture, disappear or return after death through memories, objects, places or relationships.

## Theme Transformation
A melody introduced during ordinary life may later return altered through:
- slower tempo;
- different instrumentation;
- incomplete phrasing;
- reduced harmony;
- environmental sound;
- silence;
- distant/fragmented presentation.

This lets the player feel what has changed without exposition.

## Music & Player Agency
Do not use a triumphant musical reward every time the player succeeds. Survival can feel exhausting, morally complicated or emotionally empty.

Likewise, failure does not always need ominous music. Sometimes the most devastating choice is followed by silence.

## Cultural & Artistic Range
The score may incorporate diverse instruments, textures and musical traditions when appropriate to characters and locations. Cultural influences must be used thoughtfully rather than as generic exotic decoration.

## Echo Aurora Sound
Echo Aurora receives a separate sonic identity that does not resolve into a conventional supernatural theme. It should feel recognizable, beautiful, unsettling and difficult to classify.

## Finale
The final sequence should resist over-scoring. After The Collapse ends, music can gradually recede. The observation phase should feel emotionally distant. The hospital awakening should use restrained sound. The final impossible clue should not explain itself with a giant musical sting.

## Canonical Lock
> The music should not tell the player what to feel. It should make them feel what they did not yet have words for.
