# RIDE OR DIE — WORLD VISUAL MASTER

## Canonical Direction

The eight major regions must look like places that were genuinely inhabited before The Collapse and genuinely changed afterward.

**No generic apocalypse biomes. No empty game maps. No copy-paste buildings. No basic NPC backdrop worlds.**

Each world image used for concepting, environment production, marketing, cinematics or level blocking must communicate a specific lived reality.

## 01 — THE LAST CITY

A dense modern city that still functions at the beginning of the story.

Visual requirements:
- mixed residential, commercial and civic architecture;
- believable traffic density and pedestrian behavior;
- buses, delivery vehicles, taxis, private cars and service vehicles;
- cafes, pharmacies, supermarkets, offices, schools and apartment buildings;
- pedestrians with distinct clothing, ages, occupations and purposes;
- window interiors that imply actual households and workplaces;
- imperfect streets, construction, road markings and utility infrastructure;
- gradual evidence of disruption rather than instant destruction.

After Collapse: shuttered businesses, abandoned vehicles, emergency notices, darkened windows, improvised barriers, missing services, scavenged storefronts and people adapting existing spaces.

## 02 — THE BLACK HIGHWAY

A long-distance road network rather than a single dramatic road.

Visual requirements:
- varied road surfaces and lane markings;
- service stations, roadside businesses, rest areas, farms, warehouses and maintenance yards;
- realistic road debris and vehicle breakdown patterns;
- changing weather and visibility;
- distant traffic before Collapse and isolated vehicles afterward;
- evidence of people who stopped, fled, turned back or continued.

The highway should feel dangerous because of uncertainty and human behavior, not because every frame contains wreckage.

## 03 — HOLLOWGATE

A lived-in transitional settlement assembled from existing structures rather than a fantasy fortress.

Visual requirements:
- repurposed homes and commercial buildings;
- practical fencing and controlled access;
- water collection and storage;
- cooking areas;
- sleeping spaces;
- medical corners;
- workshops;
- trade areas;
- children's spaces;
- private spaces hidden behind ordinary doors;
- visible social hierarchy without making the settlement look theatrical.

People should be doing different things simultaneously. Some work. Some rest. Some argue. Some trade. Some watch. Some avoid each other.

## 04 — THE VERDANT

A region where vegetation increasingly reclaims infrastructure.

This is not an unrealistic jungle.

Required visual logic:
- species appropriate to climate;
- weeds through pavement;
- vines around neglected structures;
- damaged drainage systems;
- flooded low points;
- agricultural remnants;
- wildlife traces;
- human paths cutting through growth;
- different stages of ecological recovery.

Human survival continues inside the ecology rather than outside it.

## 05 — THE ASHEN EXPANSE

A disaster-affected landscape shaped by heat, smoke, particulate contamination and damaged infrastructure.

Do not make every surface grey.

Use remaining original colors, faded signage, exposed materials, scorched vegetation, oxidized metal, concrete dust, damaged plastics and surviving personal belongings to preserve visual history.

The emotional effect should come from what remains visible.

## 06 — THE DROWNED

Flooded infrastructure where water changes routes and access.

Required details:
- partially submerged streets;
- floating debris with believable buoyancy;
- waterlines on buildings;
- algae and staining;
- damaged electrical infrastructure;
- boats and improvised flotation;
- altered pedestrian routes;
- reflections affected by wind and rain;
- buildings that remain inhabited above flood level.

Water must interact correctly with materials, objects and characters.

## 07 — THE SILENT NORTH

A colder region where isolation becomes a defining pressure.

Required details:
- region-specific architecture;
- heating infrastructure;
- condensation and frost;
- layered clothing;
- tire tracks and footprints;
- shortened daylight;
- indoor practical lighting;
- frozen or partially frozen water;
- evidence of people sheltering rather than simply abandoned buildings.

Silence should be visualized through low population density and distance, not through an empty world.

## 08 — THE ORIGIN

The final region should feel connected to everything that came before.

It must contain evidence of planning, infrastructure, human choices and the hidden history surrounding The Collapse.

The environment should reward observation. Architecture, materials, signage, documents, vehicles, security measures and unusual spaces should form a coherent visual trail.

It should feel real before it feels mysterious.

## NPC WORLD DENSITY

Every populated environment should contain layered human activity:

### Foreground
Characters the player can directly interact with.

### Midground
People performing contextual routines: carrying supplies, repairing objects, cooking, talking, waiting, cleaning, travelling or working.

### Background
Distant pedestrians, vehicles, animals, domestic activity, sound sources and environmental movement.

The world must not become motionless when the player stops moving.

## WORLD IMAGE GENERATION STANDARD

Every concept/world image should be generated or rendered as if it were an actual location from the final game:

- photorealistic materials;
- realistic human scale;
- natural atmospheric perspective;
- physically plausible lighting;
- authentic environmental clutter;
- non-repetitive architecture;
- believable signs and objects;
- lived-in imperfections;
- cinematic but physically grounded composition;
- no visible AI artifacts;
- no generic stock-apocalypse imagery.

## BEFORE / AFTER CONTINUITY

For every major location, retain a visual record of:

**Normal → Early Disruption → Collapse → Recovery/Adaptation.**

The player should recognize the same place even after it changes.

A supermarket remains recognizably the same supermarket after shelves are empty, doors are damaged and the building becomes a barter point.

A family home remains the same home after furniture is rearranged for sleeping, windows are covered and the kitchen becomes a ration station.

That continuity is what makes The Collapse feel like history rather than set dressing.

## Final Visual Rule

> **The world must look like it existed before the player arrived, and it must continue existing after the player leaves.**
