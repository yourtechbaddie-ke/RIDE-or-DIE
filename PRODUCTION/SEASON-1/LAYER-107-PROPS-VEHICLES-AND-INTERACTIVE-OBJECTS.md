# LAYER 107 — PROPS, VEHICLES & INTERACTIVE OBJECTS

## Purpose
Build the physical objects that make the world feel owned, used and lived in.

## Object Philosophy
Objects are not automatically loot. They can be useful, valuable, sentimental, dangerous, evidence-bearing or meaningless.

## Object Categories
- household objects;
- food and cooking equipment;
- clothing/accessories;
- medical equipment;
- tools;
- school/work items;
- phones and electronics;
- photographs and documents;
- radios and media devices;
- furniture;
- containers and storage;
- trade goods;
- weapons where appropriate;
- repair materials;
- vehicles;
- vehicle parts;
- fuel-related resources;
- settlement infrastructure.

## Distinctive Design
Objects should reflect the person, place and economic context that produced or owned them. A wealthy home, artist's studio, mechanic's workshop, hospital, school and rural farm should not share the same visual inventory.

## Luxury & Material Culture
Pre-Collapse luxury should be visible in appropriate locations through quality, craftsmanship, materials, design and ownership—not as meaningless decoration. After The Collapse, luxury objects can become:
- valuable trade assets;
- sentimental possessions;
- evidence of former status;
- impractical burdens;
- unexpectedly useful materials.

## Cultural & Artistic Objects
The world should contain authentic cultural and artistic objects across communities. Avoid tokenized decorative stereotypes. Objects should have plausible owners, histories and uses.

## Vehicles
Vehicles require individual states such as:
- owned;
- operational;
- damaged;
- abandoned;
- immobilized;
- scavenged;
- repaired;
- transferred;
- stolen;
- destroyed.

Vehicle condition affects travel, noise, visibility, fuel use, storage and risk.

## Persistent Objects
Important objects can persist after character death and move through ownership networks. A key, necklace, journal, photograph, vehicle or coat may outlive its owner.

## Interactive World Rule
The player should encounter objects because they belong to a world—not because every object exists to trigger a quest.

## Canonical Lock
> The best objects in RIDE OR DIE are the ones that make the player wonder who owned them before they found them.
