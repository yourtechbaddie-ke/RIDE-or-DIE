# RIDE OR DIE — MASTER COLOR PALETTE & WORLD VISUAL DIRECTION

## Canonical Status
**LOCKED — Production Master**

This document is the canonical color-direction reference for RIDE OR DIE. The approved palettes, their meanings, exact color values, emotional purposes, and world assignments are production-locked.

Any future environment, character, wardrobe, prop, vehicle, UI, cinematic, lighting, VFX, promotional image, or menu work must preserve this visual language unless a deliberate palette exception is approved as a new canonical direction.

---

# 01 — BRUISE & HALO

### Core statement
**“Something is wrong, but everyone is pretending it isn't.”**

### Palette
| Color | Hex | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Almost Black | `#09080D` | Deep shadow, secrecy, visual negative space |
| Bruised Plum | `#21172D` | Psychological unease, suppressed tension |
| Dead Violet | `#4B315D` | Mystery, uncertainty, emotional ambiguity |
| Tarnished Amber | `#C17A45` | Aging warmth, compromised comfort, human traces |
| Halo Gold | `#E4B878` | Hope, memory, intimacy, misleading reassurance |
| Human Bone | `#D8D0C4` | Skin/bone, worn interiors, faded materials |
| Internal Bleed | `#6B1825` | Hidden danger, injury, betrayal, emotional rupture |

### Visual identity
Bruise & Halo is the palette of **wrongness hidden inside beauty**. It avoids the obvious visual language of a conventional apocalypse. Violet carries the mystery; amber and gold create comfort; the darker reds appear only when something has already gone wrong.

The palette should make a scene feel beautiful first and suspicious second. A warm light can feel safe while actually concealing danger. A violet sky can resemble a beautiful memory while carrying an unexplained sense of dread.

### Primary uses
- Hollowgate
- character relationships and emotional scenes
- mystery-heavy interiors
- memories and psychologically ambiguous spaces
- deceptive safety
- locations where truth and appearance conflict

# 02 — TOXIC EDEN

### Core statement
**“Nature survived. Humanity didn't.”**

### Palette
| Color | Hex | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Black Moss | `#050807` | Deep forest shadow and environmental depth |
| Deep Forest | `#10251C` | Dense vegetation and living wilderness |
| Sickly Moss | `#52643A` | Unhealthy growth, decay, contamination |
| Toxic Olive | `#9A8F38` | Environmental warning and unnatural vegetation |
| Burnt Orange | `#D65F2A` | Human remnants, heat, warning, surviving civilization |
| Warning Amber | `#E7A85D` | Artificial warmth, lights, markers, human presence |
| Faded Bone | `#D8D2B8` | Weathered architecture, bones, pale organic surfaces |

### Visual identity
Toxic Eden is not simply “the green world.” It is **nature becoming dominant while the remains of humanity disappear inside it**.

Green represents the world continuing without us. Orange represents humanity refusing to disappear completely. The tension between the two should make beautiful landscapes feel subtly hostile.

Vegetation should be lush enough to feel alive, but the player should repeatedly discover abandoned structures, vehicles, homes, roads, medical facilities, or personal objects being consumed by it.

### Primary uses
- The Verdant
- overgrown structures
- contaminated or altered natural areas
- hidden settlements inside wilderness
- abandoned infrastructure
- environmental mystery

# 03 — PETROL & BLOOD

### Core statement
**“Cold truth. Warm lies.”**

### Palette
| Color | Hex | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Wet Asphalt | `#050B0D` | Night roads, deep shadow, urban surfaces |
| Petrol | `#102A30` | Cold atmosphere, technology, distance |
| Oxidized Teal | `#194C53` | Water, metal, industrial decay |
| Dried Blood | `#6D2734` | Old violence, evidence, consequence |
| Fresh Blood | `#A43C43` | Immediate danger and injury |
| Old Paper | `#D7C7B1` | Documents, photographs, human history |
| Cold Reflection | `#A7B9B4` | Glass, rain, metal, reflected light |

### Visual identity
Petrol & Blood creates a sophisticated tension between **truth and concealment**. Blue-green tones suggest distance, evidence, machinery and cold reality; the reds represent what people cannot completely hide.

This is not neon cyberpunk. The colors should remain grounded, weathered and cinematic. Red should feel like something that happened, while petrol tones feel like the cold evidence left behind.

### Primary uses
- The Black Highway
- urban night sequences
- vehicles and roadside infrastructure
- investigations
- medical and injury moments
- tense encounters
- rain, glass and reflective surfaces

# 04 — FUNERAL CREAM & WARNING ORANGE

### Core statement
**“Home is still home. Even when something terrible happened there.”**

### Palette
| Color | Hex | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Charcoal | `#0A0908` | Darkness and structural depth |
| Old Wood | `#25201C` | Homes, furniture, aging interiors |
| Weathered Brown | `#6D4A32` | Earth, timber, worn materials |
| Warning Orange | `#D46B2E` | Danger, damaged infrastructure, surviving markers |
| Window Glow | `#F0A85C` | Human habitation, warmth, distant life |
| Funeral Cream | `#E6D9C5` | Memory, domestic surfaces, faded fabric |
| Old Blood | `#8B2D25` | History, violence, loss |

### Visual identity
This palette makes abandoned places feel **personal rather than merely ruined**. The warmth of an orange window, lamp or fire should imply that somebody lived here, loved here, cooked here, fought here, or died here.

The horror comes from the contrast: a familiar domestic space can remain visually beautiful even after something terrible has happened inside it.

### Primary uses
- Hollowgate
- abandoned homes
- domestic interiors
- settlement spaces
- family memories
- safe-house environments
- evidence of former everyday life

# 05 — ASH ROSE

### Core statement
**“Love didn't die. It just became dangerous.”**

### Palette
| Color | Hex | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#0B090B` | Intimacy, darkness, emotional weight |
| Charcoal Rose | `#2A2228` | Grief, memory and restrained emotion |
| Ash Rose | `#573944` | Complicated affection and melancholy |
| Faded Rose | `#98606B` | Romance, nostalgia, tenderness |
| Old Blush | `#C48B91` | Vulnerability, skin, soft memory |
| Skin/Bone | `#D8C8BA` | Human presence and intimate close-ups |
| Wound | `#6E222E` | Love, sacrifice, betrayal and pain |

### Visual identity
Ash Rose is the emotional palette of **relationships, memory, grief and dangerous affection**. It should appear when the player is close to another person emotionally, especially when love and loss exist in the same scene.

The rose tones should never become sugary or romantic in a conventional way. They should feel weathered, mature and fragile — like an old photograph that has survived a disaster.

### Primary uses
- relationships
- romance
- family
- grief
- memories
- photographs
- intimate dialogue
- emotional cinematics

# 06 — ELECTRIC MOURNING

### Core statement
**“The world is dead, but the machines haven't noticed.”**

### Palette
| Color | Hex | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Void | `#05060A` | Absolute darkness and unknown space |
| Midnight Indigo | `#11152A` | Digital darkness and isolation |
| Bruised Violet | `#342657` | Anomalous space and psychological distortion |
| Electric Lavender | `#6256A3` | ECHO signature, impossible light |
| Dead Cyan | `#42A9A7` | Corrupted technology, screens, signal anomalies |
| Ghost Mint | `#9DD6C9` | Unnatural presence and impossible reflections |
| Corrupted Gold | `#C6A86A` | Broken systems, degraded technology, false familiarity |

### Visual identity
Electric Mourning is the visual language of **systems continuing after meaning has disappeared**.

It belongs to corrupted screens, strange broadcasts, impossible lights, abandoned facilities, encrypted systems, communications equipment and ECHO manifestations. It must remain rare enough that its appearance immediately changes the player's perception of a scene.

Electric Lavender and Ghost Mint are especially important: they should feel like colors that have entered the world from somewhere else.

### Primary uses
- ECHO
- corrupted interfaces
- secret facilities
- strange broadcasts
- impossible lights
- technological anomalies
- The Origin
- selected Silent North sequences

# 07 — HUMAN LIGHT

### Core statement
**“The palette that makes the horror hurt.”**

### Palette
| Color | Hex | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute Dark | `#030405` | Surrounding darkness |
| Charcoal | `#111315` | Neutral shadow and interior depth |
| Smoke | `#3D3A34` | Transitional surfaces and atmosphere |
| Faded Leather | `#8B7457` | Clothing, furniture, survival materials |
| Human Amber | `#D29A58` | Human warmth, fire, lamps, food and shelter |
| Candlelight | `#F1D19B` | Intimacy, hope, safety and care |
| Skin/Bone | `#F2E6D2` | Human presence and tactile realism |

### Visual identity
Human Light is the palette of **people refusing to stop being human**.

A mother feeding a child. Someone cooking at night. Friends sharing food. A survivor treating an injury. People sitting around a fire. Someone crying while another person holds them.

Warmth should exist inside darkness rather than replacing it. The darker the world becomes, the more emotionally important a small source of human light becomes.

### Primary uses
- human-centered scenes
- family
- cooking and food
- medical care
- firelight
- safe interiors
- emotional recovery
- moments of hope

# 08 — THE DROWNED

### Core statement
**“Black water + drowned gold + corpse blue.”**

### Palette
| Color | Hex | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Water | `#03080A` | Black water and abyssal depth |
| Drowned Teal | `#0D252B` | Flooded structures and water atmosphere |
| Cold Water | `#27454A` | Reflections, wet surfaces and submerged architecture |

| Fading Light | `#D4B97A` | Memory, distant illumination and remaining hope |
| Oil Black | `#171316` | Pollution, deep shadow and ruined infrastructure |

### Visual identity
The Drowned is a **beautiful graveyard of civilization**.

Water has become the dominant surface. Streets, homes, vehicles and buildings may still exist, but their original purpose is partially erased. Gold should appear in small amounts — a distant light, a rusted sign, an object, a reflection — as evidence that human civilization once occupied this place.

The region should feel quiet enough that the player becomes conscious of every sound.

### Primary uses
- The Drowned
- flooded cities
- submerged structures
- boats and water travel
- rain and reflections
- underwater or semi-submerged environments
- lost civilization

### Visual identity
The Ashen Expanse is what happens when the world has been stripped of most of its color. It must feel **vast, scorched, dry and emotionally exhausted**.

Orange no longer means ordinary danger here. It represents the last remaining heat in a damaged world — embers, distant fires, sunlight through smoke and the remnants of things that once burned.

The landscape should create scale and vulnerability. Characters should frequently appear small against enormous areas of damaged terrain.

### Primary uses
- The Ashen Expanse
- burned settlements
- scorched roads
- industrial ruins
- smoke-filled skies
- fire aftermath
- extreme environmental survival

# WORLD COLOR ASSIGNMENT — EIGHT MAJOR REGIONS

The eight major regions do not receive isolated palettes. Each world has a **dominant emotional palette** plus supporting palettes that can bleed into neighboring regions and narrative states.

## 1. THE LAST CITY
**Dominant:** Human Light

**Supporting:** Funeral Cream & Warning Orange; Petrol & Blood

**Emotional statement:** **“Civilization hasn't realized it's dying yet.”**

The Last City must remain recognizably modern, lived-in and beautiful. Warm apartment windows, cafés, hospitals, offices, traffic, rain and ordinary human routines should coexist with tiny visual inconsistencies. The Collapse should initially be something the player feels before they fully see it.

The horror comes from the ordinary becoming slightly wrong.

---

## 2. THE BLACK HIGHWAY
**Dominant:** Petrol & Blood

**Supporting:** Funeral Cream & Warning Orange; The Ashen Expanse

**Emotional statement:** **“The road is still there. The world around it isn't.”**

The Black Highway expands the scale of the game: long roads, empty lanes, abandoned vehicles, damaged infrastructure, distant skylines, petrol stations, rain, fog and enormous skies.

It should feel simultaneously like freedom and entrapment.

---

## 3. HOLLOWGATE
**Dominant:** Bruise & Halo

**Supporting:** Funeral Cream & Warning Orange; Ash Rose; Human Light

**Emotional statement:** **“Someone lives here. You're just not sure who.”**

Hollowgate is a functioning human settlement built around the remains of a damaged world. It should contain old homes, handmade repairs, gardens, children, kitchens, livestock, patched clothing, photographs and warm windows.

The settlement should feel alive enough to care about and suspicious enough to fear.

---

## 4. THE VERDANT
**Dominant:** Toxic Eden

**Supporting:** Human Light; Bruise & Halo

**Emotional statement:** **“Nature survived. Humanity didn't.”**

The Verdant is lush, overwhelming and beautiful. Vegetation consumes roads, buildings, vehicles and former human spaces. The player should repeatedly discover civilization hidden underneath the growth.

Beauty is not proof of safety.

---

## 5. THE ASHEN EXPANSE
**Dominant:** The Ashen Expanse

**Supporting:** Petrol & Blood; Funeral Cream & Warning Orange

**Emotional statement:** **“The world after colour has been burned away.”**

The Ashen Expanse is desaturated, immense and scorched. The environment should communicate exhaustion rather than simply destruction. Remaining orange light becomes the visual evidence that heat, fire and life have not completely disappeared.

---

## 6. THE DROWNED
**Dominant:** The Drowned

**Supporting:** Electric Mourning; Human Light

**Emotional statement:** **“Civilization is beneath the water now.”**

Flooded streets and submerged structures become the remains of a former civilization. Gold is used sparingly as a trace of human presence beneath black and blue-green water.

The region should feel like a drowned memory.

---

## 7. THE SILENT NORTH
**Dominant:** Drowned Blue-Grey / Human Light

**Supporting:** Electric Mourning

**Emotional statement:** **“Even sound feels dangerous here.”**

The Silent North uses cold blue-grey environments, deep shadow, muted cream and isolated human warmth. Snow should not simply appear white; it should absorb the character into enormous, quiet landscapes.

A tiny warm light in this world should have enormous emotional power.

---

## 8. THE ORIGIN
**Dominant:** Electric Mourning + Unknown

**Supporting:** Bruise & Halo; selected elements from every established palette

**Emotional statement:** **“You've finally reached the place that should not exist.”**

The Origin is where the normal rules of the visual system begin to break.

It may initially appear ancient, subterranean or archaeological before revealing technological and impossible elements. Stone, water, metal, hidden chambers, strange geometry, anomalous light and ECHO manifestations should coexist.

The Origin must eventually introduce colors and combinations that feel as though they do not belong to the ordinary world.

# ECHO — CROSS-WORLD COLOR SIGNATURE

ECHO does **not** belong to a single region.

It is the visual exception that can enter any environment.

### Locked ECHO colors
- Ghost Mint — `#9DD6C9`
- Electric Lavender — `#6256A3`
- Corrupted Gold — `#C6A86A`
- Void — `#05060A`

### ECHO rule
ECHO should never announce itself with a conventional horror filter or constant glowing effect. It should appear as a **visual contradiction**.

Examples:
- A green forest contains one lavender reflection.
- A hospital monitor displays ghost mint when no corresponding signal exists.
- A character's silhouette briefly carries impossible gold.
- A puddle reflects a color that is not physically present.
- A dark room contains one impossible mint light.
- A familiar screen displays a color combination that does not belong to its interface.

The player should learn that when these colors appear in an otherwise normal palette, **something is present that the world cannot explain**.

# MASTER COLOR PHILOSOPHY

RIDE OR DIE does not use color merely to identify locations.

> **RIDE OR DIE uses color to tell the player how they should feel about what they are seeing.**

Color is emotional narrative.

- **Humanity** = Human Amber
- **Love** = Ash Rose
- **Truth** = Petrol Blue / Cold Reflection
- **Lies** = Bruised Violet
- **Danger** = Dried Blood / Internal Bleed
- **Hope** = Halo Gold / Candlelight
- **Nature** = Toxic Moss / Toxic Olive
- **Horror** = Ink / Void / Almost Black
- **ECHO** = Ghost Mint + Electric Lavender + Corrupted Gold
- **Death** = Drowned Blue-Grey
- **Memory** = Funeral Cream / Old Paper / Faded Bone
- **Unknown** = Near-black + one impossible color

The visual system must allow two apparently contradictory emotions to exist in the same frame.

Examples:

**Warmly lit room + sickly green darkness outside**
→ “Why is that room still warm?”

**Beautiful violet sunset + empty street**
→ “Why does this feel like a memory I shouldn't have?”

**Character illuminated in gold + disturbing dialogue**
→ “Is this person actually safe?”

**Horrifying scene without red**
→ The absence of expected horror color can make the scene more frightening.

# GLOBAL PRODUCTION RULES

1. **No generic black-and-red apocalypse default.** Red is a narrative accent, not the universal environment color.
2. **No single global palette.** RIDE OR DIE must change emotionally as the player travels through the world.
3. **Palettes may overlap.** World transitions should feel organic rather than like hard color swaps.
4. **Lighting carries narrative meaning.** Warmth can represent humanity, deception, memory or false safety depending on context.
5. **ECHO breaks the rules.** Its colors should remain rare enough to feel unnatural.
6. **Human skin must remain believable.** Environmental grading may affect light, but characters must not become color-filtered mannequins.
7. **Materials must remain physically credible.** Color grading cannot replace realistic albedo, roughness, reflection, shadow and atmospheric behavior.
8. **Weather modifies the palette rather than replacing it.** Rain, fog, snow, smoke, dust and water should create variation within the established color family.
9. **Cinematics may temporarily intensify or suppress a palette** when narrative emotion requires it, but the underlying color identity remains recognizable.
10. **UI follows the same emotional system.** Home, Menu, Settings, Characters, Outfits, Vehicles, Medical, Inventory, Weapons, Dialogue, HUD and transition screens must inherit the world/emotional palette instead of becoming visually disconnected game interfaces.
11. **Palette progression must remain subtle.** The player should feel the world changing before consciously identifying why.
12. **The palette bible is canonical.** New colors should be treated as additions requiring deliberate approval, not casual substitutions.

---

# FINAL VISUAL PRINCIPLE

RIDE OR DIE should look like a world where **beauty, danger, love, uncertainty, horror and ordinary human life exist in the same frame**.

The player should never be able to rely on color alone to tell them whether something is safe.

That uncertainty is intentional.

**Mystery behind danger.**
**Honesty behind lies.**
**Uncertainty behind truth.**
**Horror side by side with humanity.**

This is the locked color identity of RIDE OR DIE.
