# LAYER 105 — AGE PROGRESSION & GENERATIONAL VISUALS

## Purpose
Make time visible. RIDE OR DIE must show that children grow, adults change, elders age and families continue.

## Age Is Not a Reskin
An age progression must change more than height. It may affect:
- facial proportions;
- body proportions;
- hairstyle;
- voice;
- posture;
- clothing;
- accessories;
- interests;
- movement;
- confidence;
- physical capability.

## Clothing & Identity Through Time
Wardrobes should evolve with age while retaining recognizable personal taste. Distinctive color palettes, fabrics, patterns, jewelry, cultural elements, artistic expression and luxury/practical choices may persist as identity markers while garments themselves change.

The goal is not generic "child / teen / adult" outfits. Each age state remains a person with a style.

## Family Continuity
When a character dies, their children or relatives do not become carbon copies. They may inherit:
- possessions;
- names or nicknames;
- skills;
- stories;
- grudges;
- photographs;
- clothing;
- family businesses;
- unfinished responsibilities.

They retain independent personalities and choices.

## Tariq St. Patrick
Tariq begins as a newborn. If the Season 1 timeline reaches later age states, production must support believable newborn → child → teen progression rather than keeping him visually static.

## Generational Visual Language
Later generations should feel connected to the same world while reflecting changing circumstances, fashion, technology, scarcity and culture.

## Production Requirement
Every age-progressing character needs a continuity sheet recording:
- age/date;
- physical changes;
- wardrobe changes;
- relationship changes;
- possessions;
- scars/injuries;
- hairstyle changes;
- voice changes;
- animation changes.

## Canonical Lock
> Time must leave fingerprints on the people who survive it.
