# LAYER 103 — CHARACTER APPEARANCE & WARDROBE

## Purpose
Define how character appearance communicates identity, class, occupation, personality and changing survival conditions without turning clothing into a static costume.

## Before The Collapse
Wardrobes should reflect ordinary life:
- work and school;
- social events;
- home routines;
- nightlife;
- faith/community roles;
- wealth and material access;
- personal taste;
- weather and geography.

Characters should have more than one outfit where their daily life reasonably requires it.

## During The Collapse
Wardrobes evolve through circumstances:
- clothing becomes dirty, damaged or weathered;
- people layer for warmth;
- practical footwear becomes valuable;
- bags and containers gain survival importance;
- personal items may be lost, traded, stolen or inherited;
- some characters preserve fashion as identity;
- others abandon appearance for practicality;
- wealth becomes visible through what someone can still protect or replace.

## Personal Style Rules
A character's style should reveal personality without becoming a caricature.

Examples of visual storytelling:
- meticulous clothing can indicate routine, pride or control;
- repaired clothing can indicate scarcity, attachment or skill;
- sentimental jewelry can preserve family identity;
- professional clothing can become impractical as society changes;
- a favorite garment can become a recurring emotional object.

## Wardrobe States
Where relevant, production should support:
1. Everyday.
2. Work/school.
3. Social/event.
4. Travel.
5. Early Collapse.
6. Extended survival.
7. Severe-weather survival.
8. Injury/recovery.
9. Settlement/community life.
10. Cinematic/finale state.

Not every character needs every state.

## Ownership & Continuity
Wardrobe is part of the possession system. A coat, bag, watch, necklace or pair of shoes can be:
- owned;
- lost;
- traded;
- inherited;
- recognized;
- recovered from an abandoned location;
- associated with a deceased person.

## Children & Age Progression
Children need age-appropriate clothing changes. Clothing must not remain identical across years of in-world progression.

Tariq St. Patrick begins as a newborn and requires believable newborn, child and later age-state planning if Season 1's timeline reaches those stages.

## Visual Quality Rule
Do not glamorize deprivation. Do not make every survivor look equally ruined. Different people have different access to shelter, washing, repair, replacement, storage and personal priorities.

## Canonical Lock
> Clothes are not skins. They are evidence of a life, and after The Collapse, evidence of what that life has survived.
