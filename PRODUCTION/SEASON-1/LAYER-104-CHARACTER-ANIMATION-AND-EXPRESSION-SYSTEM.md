# LAYER 104 — CHARACTER ANIMATION & EXPRESSION SYSTEM

## Purpose
Define the animation and facial-performance foundation required for believable human characters across ordinary life, fear, survival, grief and recovery.

## Core Principle
Characters must behave like people, not game mannequins. Animation communicates personality, physical condition, relationships and circumstance.

## Baseline Animation Set
Playable-capable characters should support, as appropriate:
- idle states;
- walking;
- running;
- sprinting;
- crouching;
- climbing;
- crouch-walking;
- turning;
- stopping;
- looking/observing;
- reaching;
- carrying;
- opening/closing;
- picking up/placing objects;
- eating/drinking;
- cooking;
- sitting/standing;
- sleeping/waking;
- entering/exiting vehicles;
- driving/passenger behavior;
- hiding;
- searching;
- helping another person;
- reacting to danger;
- injury and recovery states.

Not every action applies to every character.

## Facial Performance
Expressions should cover natural states such as:
- neutral;
- joy;
- amusement;
- affection;
- embarrassment;
- suspicion;
- concentration;
- confusion;
- fear;
- panic;
- anger;
- grief;
- exhaustion;
- pain;
- relief;
- disbelief;
- emotional shutdown.

## Contextual Performance
The same character should not display identical reactions in every circumstance. A person may hide fear from a stranger, break down alone, joke under pressure, freeze during danger or become unexpectedly calm.

## Physical Condition
Animation should communicate:
- fatigue;
- hunger;
- dehydration;
- injury;
- illness;
- stress;
- cold/heat;
- carrying load;
- recovery.

These states must not become exaggerated status effects. They are embodied storytelling and gameplay feedback.

## Relationship Performance
Characters should recognize familiar people through:
- eye contact;
- distance;
- posture;
- hesitation;
- touch where appropriate;
- avoidance;
- protective positioning;
- grief;
- relief at reunion.

## Death & Loss
Death sequences must prioritize emotional and narrative consequence rather than spectacle. Where appropriate, other characters should react differently based on relationship, personality and circumstances.

## Camera Compatibility
Animations must work in:
- normal gameplay;
- close third-person framing where used;
- cinematic sequences;
- dialogue scenes;
- environmental observation;
- transition moments.

## No Emotional Looping
Avoid repeating a single generic fear, grief or happiness animation for all characters. Performance libraries should allow personality-specific variation.

## Canonical Lock
> In RIDE OR DIE, the player should be able to read a person's feelings before the person says a word.
