# LAYER 108 — AUDIO MASTER BIBLE

## Purpose

Establish the complete sound identity of RIDE OR DIE. Audio is a core gameplay, world-simulation and storytelling system—not a finishing layer.

The new production standard is **naturalistic, cinematic and spatially believable**. The player should hear a world populated by real people, real materials and real physical spaces.

## Audio Pillars

1. Human presence.
2. Environmental realism.
3. Spatial accuracy.
4. Survival awareness.
5. Emotional storytelling.
6. Cinematic tension.
7. Intentional silence.
8. Dynamic world continuity.
9. ECHO ambiguity.

## Required Sound Families

- footsteps by surface, footwear, weight, pace and emotional state;
- clothing movement by fabric, layering and body movement;
- breathing by exertion, fear, injury, cold, heat and fatigue;
- swallowing, throat clearing, coughs, sneezes and small involuntary sounds where appropriate;
- pain, panic, crying, laughter and restrained emotional reactions;
- doors, locks, hinges, windows, keys and handles with material-specific resonance;
- cooking, dishes, cutlery, refrigerators, taps, kettles, appliances and domestic activity;
- phones, vibration motors, notifications, charging, typing and damaged electronics;
- vehicles differentiated by engine, age, condition, surface, speed and interior/exterior position;
- traffic, distant movement and urban sound beds;
- tools, repairs, machinery and improvised survival equipment;
- weather, wind pressure, rain on different materials, thunder and environmental exposure;
- water movement, drainage, splashing and submerged acoustics;
- fire, smoke and structural creaking;
- wildlife and insects appropriate to each region;
- crowds and settlement ambience with individual conversations rather than one looping crowd sound;
- radio, television, emergency broadcasts and media degradation;
- medical environments and equipment;
- debris, glass, metal, wood, concrete, plastic and fabric interaction;
- object handling with weight-aware variations;
- UI/gameplay feedback that remains subtle and diegetically appropriate.

## Human Audio Realism

Recurring characters must not sound like actors reading isolated lines into a microphone.

Performance direction should include:

- conversational pacing;
- hesitation;
- breaths before difficult statements;
- interruptions;
- overlapping speech;
- unfinished sentences;
- changes in volume caused by distance and emotion;
- nervous laughter;
- silence;
- swallowing and recovery after crying;
- fatigue and breathlessness;
- speech affected by injury or environmental conditions when narratively appropriate.

A character's emotional state must affect delivery without turning every scene into melodrama.

## Dialogue Recording / AI Voice Direction

Where AI-assisted voice production is used, prompts and generation notes must specify:

**age + emotional state + physical condition + social relationship + scene context + speaking distance + intensity + natural hesitation.**

Do not generate every line as clean studio speech. Dialogue needs controlled imperfection to preserve human realism.

Never use an identical voice profile for multiple major characters.

## Spatial Audio

Every important sound should have a plausible source.

The mix should communicate:

- left/right position;
- front/behind;
- distance;
- obstruction;
- room size;
- surface reflections;
- indoor/outdoor transition;
- elevation;
- vehicle interior/exterior;
- weather exposure.

A person behind a closed door should not sound like a person standing beside the player.

## Environmental Sound Layers

Every location should use multiple simultaneous layers:

### Layer A — Distant world
Traffic, weather, infrastructure, wildlife, city/region bed.

### Layer B — Local space
Nearby machinery, appliances, conversations, movement, water, wind and architecture.

### Layer C — Immediate physical interaction
Player footsteps, clothing, breathing, object handling and nearby NPC actions.

### Layer D — Narrative events
Dialogue, vehicles approaching, alarms, breaking structures, broadcasts and discoveries.

### Layer E — Psychological/ECHO layer
Only when narratively justified. This layer must never become a constant horror soundtrack.

## Pre-Collapse Sound

The world should sound inhabited and inconvenient in ordinary ways:

- traffic lights and pedestrian crossings;
- buses braking;
- distant arguments;
- restaurant dishes;
- school corridors;
- office printers and keyboards;
- supermarket refrigeration;
- apartment plumbing;
- elevators;
- delivery vehicles;
- music leaking from shops and homes;
- television through walls;
- phones and notifications;
- people cooking, cleaning, laughing and moving through homes.

The objective is to make normal life so convincing that its gradual disappearance is emotionally noticeable.

## Collapse Transition

Sound progressively changes:

**busy → irregular → disrupted → sparse → tense → dangerous → unnaturally quiet → survival ambience.**

Do not simply mute civilization after a scripted disaster. Individual systems fail at different rates.

A city may lose traffic before losing generators. A neighborhood may retain water after losing power. A settlement may become noisy because people begin cooking outdoors. Different regions should therefore develop different acoustic identities.

## Silence

Silence is an authored state.

Silence should have texture: distant wind, room tone, breathing, clothing movement, structural creaks or very distant activity may remain audible.

True near-silence should be reserved for moments where its absence of ordinary sound is itself meaningful.

## Audio as Gameplay Information

Players may use sound to detect:

- nearby people;
- vehicles;
- movement behind cover;
- approaching footsteps;
- changing weather;
- settlement activity;
- distant conflict;
- structural instability;
- hidden events;
- water movement;
- machinery;
- whether a location is genuinely occupied.

Sound should reward attentive players without becoming an artificial radar system.

## Dynamic NPC Audio

NPC sound must reflect autonomous activity.

If the player enters a settlement at night, they may hear different combinations of:

- cooking;
- quiet conversation;
- children being settled;
- doors closing;
- generators;
- someone repairing equipment;
- footsteps moving between shelters;
- coughing;
- distant watch activity.

The mix changes according to time, weather, population, resource conditions, recent events and settlement state.

## Region Audio Identities

### The Last City
Traffic, appliances, voices, commercial music, public announcements, elevators, construction and layered urban density.

### The Black Highway
Engine noise, tire noise, wind, roadside businesses, distant trucks, insects, weather and long periods of exposed road silence.

### Hollowgate
Human-scale domestic sound: cooking, tools, children, conversation, water containers, generators, footsteps and controlled perimeter activity.

### The Verdant
Wind through vegetation, insects, birds, water, distant human paths and the acoustic contrast between reclaimed nature and occupied structures.

### The Ashen Expanse
Dry wind, particulate movement, damaged structures, distant mechanical sounds and reduced wildlife activity where narratively justified.

### The Drowned
Water against buildings, floating debris, pumps, boats, rain, echoes across flooded spaces and altered movement acoustics.

### The Silent North
Cold wind, snow/frost movement, enclosed heating systems, footsteps on frozen surfaces, sparse wildlife and distant settlement sounds.

### The Origin
A deliberately controlled acoustic identity built from recognizable elements of earlier regions, gradually revealing that the world has been connected all along.

## ECHO Audio Identity

ECHO remains separate from The Collapse.

Its sound must be subtle enough that players can initially mistake it for a damaged electronic device, radio interference, environmental resonance or audio artifact.

Possible signatures:

- impossible stereo displacement;
- sounds appearing slightly before their visual cause;
- reversed or corrupted fragments used sparingly;
- familiar environmental sounds with one physically impossible property;
- low-level signal instability;
- the recurring four-note motif;
- radio voices that seem to answer events they should not know about.

ECHO must never become a predictable monster cue.

## Music & Score Relationship

Music should not fill every emotional space.

Use score selectively. Let environmental audio carry ordinary life, tension and grief whenever possible.

Before Collapse, music can emerge naturally from radios, shops, cars, homes and social spaces. Later, score becomes more intentional and isolated.

## Audio Asset Naming Standard

Use:

`ROD_[REGION]_[SYSTEM]_[EVENT]_[SURFACE/STATE]_[VARIANT]`

Examples:

`ROD_LASTCITY_FOOTSTEP_CONCRETE_WET_03`

`ROD_HOLLOWGATE_GENERATOR_IDLE_NIGHT_02`

`ROD_DROWNED_WATER_BUILDING_IMPACT_01`

`ROD_ECHO_RADIO_RESPONSE_04`

## Audio QA

Reject any asset that sounds:

- excessively synthetic;
- looped too obviously;
- detached from its environment;
- identical to another character's performance;
- physically impossible for the source;
- over-compressed;
- dramatically over-scored;
- generic enough to identify it as stock/game audio.

## Accessibility

Production must support:

- subtitles/captions;
- speaker identification where useful;
- directional audio indicators for critical gameplay information;
- visual equivalents for essential audio events;
- adjustable dialogue/music/SFX/ambience levels;
- accessibility options that preserve narrative information.

## Canonical Lock

> **RIDE OR DIE should sound like a world full of people before it sounds like a game.**

The player's strongest audio memory should not be a loud explosion. It should be the feeling that someone was cooking in a room nearby, a car passed in the distance, someone hesitated before answering, and life continued—until it didn't.
