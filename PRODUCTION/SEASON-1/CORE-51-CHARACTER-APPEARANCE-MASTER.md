# RIDE OR DIE — CORE 51 CHARACTER APPEARANCE MASTER

## Status
**Production reference — appearance identities locked for visual development.**

## Purpose
This file establishes a unique, reusable visual identity for every entry in the current 51-character ensemble registry. It is the appearance source to consult before portraits, full-body references, turnarounds, cinematics, gameplay models and wardrobe variants are created.

The visual system follows `LAYER-101-CHARACTER-VISUAL-BIBLE.md`: identity must survive wardrobe changes, stress, weathering and the Collapse. Clothing communicates occupation, personality, class and circumstances rather than functioning as a permanent costume.

### Canon discipline
- Existing canon is preserved where the repository already establishes ethnicity/background, occupation, signature possessions or other appearance facts.
- Where canon is silent, the visual choice below is a **production design choice**, not retroactive biography.
- No two characters intentionally share the same combination of face shape, hair, silhouette, wardrobe language and recognition anchor.
- `Protagonist` is retained only because it remains in the current registry; the production bible states that the player has no canonical physical identity. Do **not** render a fixed protagonist body unless a future canon decision replaces this placeholder.
- `ECHO` is not human and must never be rendered as an ordinary human cast member.
- Collapse clothing evolves from the person's pre-Collapse wardrobe. Do not put everyone in identical "survivor" clothing.

## Wardrobe continuity rules
1. **Pre-Collapse:** dress characters according to their actual occupation, income, age, geography and personality.
2. **Early Collapse:** preserve recognizable pieces while adding practical layers, improvised storage and weather protection.
3. **Later survival:** repair and recombine existing clothing; do not replace the whole cast with identical tactical outfits.
4. **Recognition:** face, hair, body silhouette, posture and one or two signature anchors must remain readable even when clothing changes.
5. **Damage:** dirt, sweat, injury, missing buttons, torn seams and weathering should be scene-specific and cumulative.
6. **Cultural accuracy:** where a character's established background affects dress, use culturally appropriate everyday choices rather than stereotypes.
7. **Age accuracy:** children, teenagers and elders must retain age-appropriate proportions and clothing behavior.
8. **No cloning:** a shared occupation does not justify shared face structure, haircut, silhouette or outfit formula.
