# LAYER 113 — GAMEPLAY ANIMATION & INTERACTION

## Purpose

RIDE OR DIE must feel like an actual playable world, not a sequence of cinematics. Human animation, object interaction and environmental response connect the cinematic story to player agency.

## 1. Core Player Interactions

The interaction framework supports context-sensitive actions including:

- opening and closing doors
- picking up and placing objects
- searching containers
- examining evidence
- using phones and radios
- cooking and preparing food
- eating and drinking
- washing and cleaning
- changing clothing
- carrying supplies
- climbing
- hiding
- driving
- repairing
- treating injuries
- helping another person
- moving furniture or obstacles
- interacting with vehicles
- reading documents and diaries

## 2. Animation Must Reflect Context

The same interaction should not look identical in every circumstance.

A character may move differently when:

- healthy
- exhausted
- injured
- frightened
- carrying weight
- protecting someone
- moving quietly
- rushing
- hiding
- travelling through difficult terrain

## 3. Clothing & Physical Continuity

Gameplay animation must support the established wardrobe system.

Characters can have multiple outfits and rotate clothing naturally. Clothing may become:

- dirty
- sweaty
- wet
- stained
- damaged
- repaired
- replaced

Fresh clothing after washing, changing, shopping or reaching a functioning settlement should visibly restore a sense of normality where circumstances allow.

No recurring named character should be permanently locked to one outfit merely for asset convenience.

## 4. Everyday Life

Optional everyday interactions may include:

- showering
- laundry
- cooking
- eating
- watching television
- sleeping
- shopping
- working
- studying
- socializing
- cleaning

These are not mandatory chore loops. Their purpose is to make ordinary life believable and to show how those same activities transform during the Collapse.

## 5. Object Response

Objects should communicate physicality. Doors have resistance, containers have states, clothing moves with bodies, vehicles respond to condition, and carried items affect movement when appropriate.

## 6. Social Interaction

Characters can:

- greet
- hug
- comfort
- argue
- avoid
- hand objects to one another
- assist injured people
- share food
- dance or celebrate
- react physically to danger
- mourn

Relationship context determines animation choices.

## 7. Stealth & Pursuit

Hide-and-hunt gameplay depends on readable movement, visibility, sound, line of sight and environmental interaction. Players can observe, prepare, evade, misdirect, hide, flee or confront.

## 8. Death & Continuation

Death animation and aftermath must connect to the permanent world-state system. A death is not erased when the player changes playable perspective.

## 9. Cinematic Compatibility

Gameplay animation should transition cleanly into cinematic camera language without making characters suddenly behave like different people.

## 10. Canonical Rule

> Every important interaction should make the player feel that they are controlling a person moving through a physical world — not selecting an animation from a menu.
