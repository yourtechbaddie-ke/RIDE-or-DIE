# RIDE OR DIE — WORLD VISUAL DESCRIPTIONS

## Purpose
This document is the visual-generation master brief for the RIDE OR DIE universe.

These descriptions are intentionally much more specific than ordinary environment prompts. Every world image must communicate **place, history, human behavior, physical reality and atmosphere** simultaneously.

The target is not "an impressive game background."

The target is a believable place in a realistic world where the player happens to be standing.

---

# GLOBAL VISUAL STANDARD

All world renders should target:

**photorealistic AAA survival-game environment, cinematic but physically grounded, physically based materials, realistic global illumination, natural atmospheric perspective, believable architecture, authentic human scale, imperfect surfaces, contextual clutter, realistic weather interaction, subtle environmental storytelling, natural depth, high-detail geometry, realistic vegetation, believable object placement, documentary photography sensibility, premium console/PC game quality.**

### Absolutely avoid
- generic AI apocalypse scenery
- identical abandoned cars repeated throughout a scene
- perfectly symmetrical streets
- empty streets with nothing happening
- excessive rubble everywhere
- artificial fog used to hide weak detail
- neon cyberpunk aesthetics unless explicitly required
- plastic-looking materials
- fantasy architecture without narrative justification
- mannequin-like people
- identical NPC faces or clothing
- people posing for the camera
- perfectly clean rooms
- random clutter with no contextual reason
- excessive blood/gore as a substitute for storytelling
- obvious AI artifacts
- "concept art" appearance

# 01 — THE LAST CITY

## Visual identity
A contemporary major city that was completely ordinary before everything changed. It must be recognizable as a functioning human city first and a disaster environment second.

### Daytime
Wide streets with realistic traffic patterns, apartment towers, low-rise businesses, pharmacies, cafés, schools, offices, bus stops, delivery vehicles, pedestrians and ordinary signage. Windows should reveal different lives: curtains open in some apartments, lights on in others, desks, kitchens, plants and personal belongings.

The city should contain wealth differences naturally: renovated storefronts beside aging buildings, expensive vehicles alongside old commuter cars, polished office districts beside neglected side streets.

### Early Collapse
Do not immediately turn the city into a wasteland. Show subtle social failure:

- supermarket queues
- partially empty shelves
- overloaded pharmacies
- people checking phones
- emergency notices taped over ordinary posters
- police redirecting traffic
- delivery delays
- commuters carrying bags home
- businesses closing early
- apartment residents watching streets from windows
- confused conversations

The most powerful image is one where **normal life and crisis exist in the same frame**.

### Late Collapse
Traffic becomes irregular. Some intersections work, others do not. Streetlights fail selectively. Vehicles are parked at strange angles because people abandoned them in a hurry. Businesses are shuttered individually rather than uniformly.

Show evidence of evacuation without turning every building into rubble.

### Interior language
Apartments should reveal personality through furniture, photographs, children's drawings, laundry, dishes, books, chargers, groceries, work equipment and unfinished routines.

# 02 — THE BLACK HIGHWAY

## Visual identity
A long-distance road system gradually transformed from infrastructure into a survival corridor.

### Highway
Multiple generations of vehicles: family SUVs, delivery vans, taxis, motorcycles, buses, old sedans and commercial trucks. Some are maintained. Some are damaged. Some have improvised repairs.

Road markings should fade naturally. Guardrails should have individual damage. Tire tracks should respond to weather and traffic.

### Service stations
Not generic abandoned gas stations. Each station should have its own history:

- different signage
- different shop layouts
- different levels of vandalism
- remaining merchandise
- repair equipment
- staff areas
- fuel infrastructure
- personal belongings

### Survivor activity
People repair vehicles, trade supplies, search abandoned cars, cook beside vehicles, sleep inside vans, stand lookout, argue about routes and help strangers.

Nobody should look like they are waiting for the player.

### Night
Headlights become important sources of illumination. Small groups gather around practical light sources. Distant vehicles move through darkness. The road should feel enormous and exposed.

# 03 — HOLLOWGATE

## Visual identity
Hollowgate is not a stereotypical apocalypse camp. It is a settlement created by ordinary people adapting existing infrastructure into a functioning community.

### Architecture
Repurposed warehouses, houses, workshops, storage rooms, temporary sleeping areas, community kitchens, water points, trading spaces and reinforced entrances.

Construction should look improvised but intelligent: reused timber, welded metal, repaired doors, tarps, salvaged shelving, hand-painted signs and practical lighting.

### Human life
This is crucial.

Show people doing ordinary things:

- preparing food
- washing clothes
- repairing shoes
- carrying water
- cleaning tools
- arguing quietly
- comforting children
- trading
- reading
- checking a radio
- tending injuries
- sitting alone after exhausting work

A settlement feels real when not everyone is performing survival simultaneously.

### Emotional visual language
Warmth exists alongside fear. Some spaces are carefully maintained because people need normality. Other spaces are neglected because nobody has the energy.

### Night
Generators, candles, battery lamps and cooking fires create pools of practical light. Avoid theatrical orange apocalypse lighting everywhere.

# 04 — THE VERDANT

## Visual identity
Nature is reclaiming infrastructure, but the environment must remain ecologically believable.

Roots break pavement in specific locations. Moisture creates moss on shaded surfaces. Vines follow structures where light and support allow them. Trees grow through damaged buildings only where years of neglect make that plausible.

### Visual composition
Contrast beautiful vegetation with human infrastructure:

- overgrown intersections
- submerged road signs
- cracked sidewalks
- abandoned playgrounds
- vegetation entering apartments
- animals moving through empty streets
- rusting fences disappearing into growth

### Human traces
Occasional paths show where survivors still travel. Cut vegetation, footprints, improvised bridges, recently opened doors and collected resources reveal that humans remain present.

# 05 — THE ASHEN EXPANSE

## Visual identity
A disaster-altered region where heat, particulate residue and infrastructure damage have reshaped daily life.

Do not make it a single flat gray landscape.

Use variation in concrete, brick, glass, metal, scorched vegetation, exposed earth and remaining painted surfaces.

### Atmosphere
Visibility changes with wind. Fine particles accumulate in sheltered places. Sunlight becomes harsh through suspended dust. Surfaces retain different heat and weathering characteristics.

### Human adaptation
Survivors cover skin and breathing pathways when conditions demand it, create shade, protect water supplies and modify vehicles and buildings to cope with the environment.

Their equipment should look used and repaired rather than factory-new.

---

# 06 — THE DROWNED

## Visual identity
Flooding creates a new geography rather than simply placing water over an existing map.

### Water behavior
Water levels differ by elevation. Low streets flood first. Underground areas become inaccessible. Buildings show different flood lines. Floating debris collects according to wind and current.

### Architecture
Mold, staining, swollen wood, corroded metal and damaged electrical systems appear according to exposure.

### Survivor infrastructure
Raised walkways, boats, improvised platforms, ropes, elevated storage and waterproof containers should appear where people have had time to adapt.

### Human realism
People move differently around water. They test surfaces, carry loads carefully, help children across crossings and avoid visibly unsafe structures.

---

# 07 — THE SILENT NORTH

## Visual identity
A cold region where environment changes behavior.

### Exterior
Snow accumulation follows roofs, ledges, vehicles and wind direction. Footpaths become compacted where people repeatedly walk. Ice forms differently on shaded and exposed surfaces.

### Interior
Interiors become visually important: condensation, wet clothing, drying areas, heating equipment, blankets, food storage, boots, gloves and improvised insulation.

### Human behavior
People conserve movement and heat. Conversations happen closer to heat sources. Doors are opened quickly. Clothing is layered according to activity.

The environment should feel quiet because sound is naturally absorbed by snow and distance—not because the scene has been given a generic silence filter.

---

# 08 — THE ORIGIN

## Visual identity
The Origin should initially look almost unnervingly intact.

Architecture is controlled, maintained and deliberate. Surfaces are cleaner. Systems appear operational. Lighting is consistent. Objects are organized.

But something is subtly wrong.

### Visual clues
Use repetition in architecture, unusual maintenance patterns, restricted access points, unexplained infrastructure, surveillance-like positioning and objects that connect visually to places the player has already visited.

Never reveal the mystery through obvious horror imagery.

The player should feel **"this place was designed for something"** before understanding what that something is.

# HUMAN NPC VISUAL STANDARD

NPCs are part of the world simulation, not decoration.

Every visible person should have:

- believable facial asymmetry
- natural skin texture
- realistic hair and grooming
- individual clothing combinations
- varied body proportions
- context-appropriate footwear
- realistic posture
- subtle signs of fatigue, age or occupation
- objects that relate to their current activity

### Behavior in visual references
People should be captured mid-action rather than posing:

- talking while looking elsewhere
- carrying something
- checking a phone
- fixing an object
- watching a child
- eating
- walking with different rhythms
- sitting asymmetrically
- resting after physical effort
- avoiding eye contact
- reacting to another person

### Social realism
Groups should contain relationships. A parent behaves differently around a child. Friends occupy space differently from strangers. Someone nervous may repeatedly check an exit. A tired worker may lean against a wall. Two people arguing should not both perform exaggerated anger.

---

# CINEMATIC CAMERA STANDARD

World images should be generated as if captured from an actual game camera or premium location photography:

- believable eye-level perspective
- physically plausible lens behavior
- realistic depth of field when appropriate
- natural exposure
- realistic shadow direction
- no floating objects
- no impossible architecture
- no excessive wide-angle distortion

Establishing shots should communicate geography. Mid shots should communicate human activity. Close environmental shots should reveal evidence of personal history.

---

# FINAL QUALITY TEST

Before accepting any world visual, ask:

**1. Could this location physically exist?**

**2. Can I tell what people used the space for?**

**3. Can I see evidence of lives that existed before the player arrived?**

**4. Do the people look like individuals rather than NPC templates?**

**5. Does weather affect materials and behavior?**

**6. Is the destruction selective and believable?**

**7. Would this still look believable if the supernatural elements were removed?**

**8. Does the image tell a story without explaining it?**

If the answer to any of these is no, regenerate or revise the asset.

## RIDE OR DIE visual principle

> **The Collapse changed the world. It did not erase the fact that human beings lived there.**

That principle should be visible in every street, room, settlement, vehicle, object and person in the game.
